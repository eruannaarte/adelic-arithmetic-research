# Arithmetic Observability II

## Minimal harmonic completion of an arithmetic blind space

**Research manuscript — 15 August 2026**

### Status

The algebraic theorems in Sections 2--5 are proved.  The canonical numerical
design in Section 6 is accompanied by an independent Arb interval certificate
and adversarial verifier tests.  Computed diagnostics are labelled separately
from proved statements.  The model remains a finite arithmetic harmonic toy
model; no claim about an unknown physical substrate is made.

---

## Abstract

Prime-resonant Dirichlet traces recover every complementary valuation
marginal of a finite prime-exponent array, but they are exactly blind to its
top tensor interaction.  For `r` prime axes with `s` exponent states, this
blind space has dimension

\[
 k=(s-1)^r.
\]

This paper determines the minimum number of ordinary complex harmonic traces
needed to remove that obstruction.  Over complex coefficients, exactly `k`
additional traces are necessary and almost every `k`-tuple of times is
sufficient.  Over real coefficients, each complex reading supplies two real
quadratures: the sharp generic threshold is

\[
 \boxed{\left\lceil\frac{(s-1)^r}{2}\right\rceil}.
\]

The sufficiency proof is not dimension counting.  A tensor factorization
reduces the hidden interaction to distinct prime-log frequencies, after which
a paired cosine--sine Vandermonde argument constructs a nonzero maximal
minor.  Rank-deficient schedules therefore form a nowhere-dense,
Lebesgue-null real-analytic set in every open time window.

A second construction uses Kronecker density to approximate a tensor Fourier
grid.  It shows that the complex completion operator can be made arbitrarily
close to an isometry on the blind space, separating mere generic
identifiability from deliberate stable design.

For the canonical box generated by `(2,3,5)` with four exponent states, the
blind dimension is 27.  Thirteen complex readings are impossible; fourteen
are sufficient.  An explicit rational schedule

\[
 t_m=\frac{1279m}{100},\qquad 1\le m\le14,
\]

is certified directly from raw traces.  The certificate proves a positive
restricted interaction floor and a positive floor for the complete 62-trace
raw operator, without inverse-Vandermonde calibration.  Thus the obstruction,
the minimum acquisition count, and a quantitative distinguishability
geometry are all explicit in one nontrivial arithmetic model.

## 1. The question forced by Arithmetic Observability I

Arithmetic Observability I considered the finite trace

\[
 F_c(t)=\sum_{\alpha\in\{0,\ldots,s-1\}^r}
 c_\alpha\exp\!\left(-it\sum_{j=1}^r\alpha_j\log p_j\right),
 \tag{1.1}
\]

where `r>=1`, `s>=2`, and `p_1,...,p_r` are distinct known primes.  At times

\[
 t_{j,m}=\frac{2\pi m}{\log p_j},
 \qquad 0\le m<s^{r-1},
 \tag{1.2}
\]

the `p_j` phase resonates.  A square Vandermonde inversion then recovers the
complementary `p_j`-valuation marginal.  Pooling all axes determines every
ANOVA component except

\[
 K=W_1\otimes\cdots\otimes W_r,
 \qquad
 W_j=\left\{x\in\mathbb F^s:\sum_{a=0}^{s-1}x_a=0\right\},
 \tag{1.3}
\]

where `F` is `R` or `C`.  Hence

\[
 \dim_{\mathbb F}K=(s-1)^r.
 \tag{1.4}
\]

That exact kernel is an obstruction, not a numerical accident.  It leaves a
specific acquisition problem:

> How many nonresonant values of the same global harmonic trace are needed to
> observe `K`, and what stability can those values provide under a declared
> raw-sample noise norm?

The answer depends on the coefficient field.  A complex-linear reading has
one complex rank over `C`, but two real quadratures over `R`.  Confusing those
models changes the sharp threshold by almost a factor of two.

## 2. Completion is exactly a restriction problem

Let

\[
 H_{\mathbb F}=(\mathbb F^s)^{\otimes r}
\]

and let

\[
 \mathcal R_{\mathbb F}:H_{\mathbb F}\longrightarrow Y_0
\]

denote either the pooled raw resonant operator or an invertibly calibrated
version.  We assume every prime axis is retained.  Arithmetic Observability I
then gives

\[
 \ker\mathcal R_{\mathbb F}=K_{\mathbb F}.
 \tag{2.1}
\]

For a tuple `T=(t_1,...,t_m)`, define

\[
 E_Tc=(F_c(t_1),\ldots,F_c(t_m)).
 \tag{2.2}
\]

The augmented acquisition is

\[
 \mathcal A_T=(\mathcal R_{\mathbb F},E_T).
 \tag{2.3}
\]

The elementary identity

\[
 \ker\mathcal A_T
 =K_{\mathbb F}\cap\ker E_T
 =\ker(E_T|_{K_{\mathbb F}})
 \tag{2.4}
\]

is the organizing observation of this paper.  The original 64-dimensional
problem need not be rediscovered from scratch.  Completion is equivalent to
making the new traces injective on the known 27-dimensional obstruction.

This restriction is an analysis device.  A physical trace returns `E_Tc`, not
`E_TP_Kc`; the observable `K^perp` component is still present.  Section 6
therefore certifies both the restricted operator and the complete raw
operator.

### 2.1 A concrete basis and its metric

Put `q=s-1` and choose

\[
 b_a=e_a-e_0,\qquad 1\le a\le q.
\]

Then

\[
 h_\beta=\bigotimes_{j=1}^r b_{\beta_j},
 \qquad \beta\in\{1,\ldots,q\}^r,
 \tag{2.5}
\]

is a basis of `K`.  Direct factorization gives

\[
 F_{h_\beta}(t)
 =g_\beta(t)
 :=\prod_{j=1}^r
 \left(p_j^{-i\beta_jt}-1\right).
 \tag{2.6}
\]

The basis is not orthonormal.  Its source Gram matrix is

\[
 S_K=(I_q+\mathbf1\mathbf1^*)^{\otimes r}.
 \tag{2.7}
\]

Consequently, any stability floor computed in these coordinates must be a
generalized eigenvalue relative to `S_K`.  An ordinary singular value of the
coordinate matrix is not an intrinsic coefficient-space constant.

## 3. The exact complex threshold

In this section `H=H_C`, so every trace is one complex-linear scalar.

### Theorem 3.1 — minimal generic complex completion

Let `k=(s-1)^r`.

1. If `m<k`, then `A_T` is noninjective for every choice of `m` times.
2. If `m=k`, define

   \[
   \mathscr D(T)
   =\det\left[g_\beta(t_\ell)\right]_{{1\le\ell\le k}
   ,\,{\beta\in\{1,\ldots,s-1\}^r}}.
   \tag{3.1}
   \]

   Then

   \[
   \boxed{\mathcal A_T\text{ is injective}
   \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
   \mathscr D(T)\ne0.}
   \tag{3.2}
   \]

3. In every nonempty open interval `I`, the bad set

   \[
   \{T\in I^k:\mathscr D(T)=0\}
   \tag{3.3}
   \]

   is closed, nowhere dense, and Lebesgue null.

Thus `k` is the exact generic threshold on the full complex coefficient
space.

#### Proof

If `m<k`, rank--nullity applied to the complex-linear map
\(E_T|_K:K\to\mathbb C^m\) leaves a nonzero kernel.

For `m=k`, equation (2.6) is precisely the matrix of the restriction, proving
(3.2).  It remains to show that its determinant is not identically zero.
Expand

\[
 g_\beta(t)=
 \sum_{S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,r\}}
 (-1)^{r-|S|}
 \exp\!\left(-it\sum_{j\in S}\beta_j\log p_j\right).
 \tag{3.4}
\]

In a relation `sum_beta a_beta g_beta(t)=0`, consider the full-support
frequency

\[
 \omega_\beta=\sum_{j=1}^r\beta_j\log p_j
 =\log\prod_{j=1}^rp_j^{\beta_j}.
 \tag{3.5}
\]

Unique factorization shows that this frequency occurs only in the
full-support term of `g_beta`: it cannot occur in a proper subset, which has
zero exponent on an omitted prime, or for another multi-index.  Distinct
finite exponentials are linearly independent, so every `a_beta` is zero.
The functions `g_beta` are therefore linearly independent.

Their evaluation vectors on any open interval span \(\mathbb C^k\); otherwise a
nonzero analytic linear combination would vanish on an interval and hence
identically.  Some `k` evaluations are independent.  Finally,
\(\lvert\mathscr D\rvert^2\) is a nonzero real-analytic function, whose zero
set is closed, nowhere dense, and null.  This applies inside every open
interval. \(\square\)

### 3.1 Generic is not uniformly stable

Good tuples may approach a tuple with repeated times or a resonant row.
Their smallest completion singular value then tends to zero.  Thus
almost-everywhere injectivity supplies no uniform noise radius.  Each concrete
schedule still needs a conditioning proof.

## 4. The exact real threshold

Now let `H=H_R`.  Realify one complex trace as

\[
 \mathfrak e_t(c)=
 \begin{pmatrix}\Re F_c(t)\\ \Im F_c(t)\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb R^2,
 \tag{4.1}
\]

and stack these paired rows into \(\mathfrak E_T\).  Let

\[
 \mathfrak A_T=(\mathcal R_{\mathbb R},\mathfrak E_T).
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — minimal generic real completion

With `k=(s-1)^r`, the following statements hold.

1. If `2m<k`, every `m`-time augmented operator has a nonzero real kernel.
2. If

   \[
   m=\left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil,
   \tag{4.2}
   \]

   then almost every \(T\in I^m\) gives an injective augmented operator in
   every nonempty open interval `I`.

Hence

\[
 \boxed{m_{\mathbb R}^{\rm generic}
 =\left\lceil\frac{(s-1)^r}{2}\right\rceil.}
 \tag{4.3}
\]

If `k=2m`, the realified restriction is generically square and nonsingular.
If `k=2m-1`, it is generically `2m` by `2m-1` with full column rank; one real
quadrature is redundant at the threshold.

#### Necessity

The restriction maps a `k`-dimensional real space into `R^{2m}`.  If
`2m<k`, rank--nullity and (2.4) give an exact collision. \(\square\)

Sufficiency requires more than counting, because the two rows supplied at
one time are paired by the same phase curve.  The next lemma resolves that
constraint.

### Lemma 4.2 — paired cosine--sine Vandermonde

Let `mu_1,...,mu_k` be distinct real numbers and form the realified evaluation
matrix whose paired rows at `t_l` are

\[
 (\cos(\mu_jt_\ell))_{j=1}^k,
 \qquad
 (\sin(\mu_jt_\ell))_{j=1}^k.
 \tag{4.4}
\]

For `m=ceil(k/2)`, there exist `m` arbitrarily small positive times for which
this matrix has column rank `k`.

#### Proof

First suppose `k=2m`.  Choose positive `u_1,...,u_m` with distinct squares and
put `t_l=epsilon u_l`.  In the determinant expansion, cosine rows contain
only even powers of `mu_j`, while sine rows contain only odd powers.  The
lowest nonzero coefficient uses powers

\[
 0,2,\ldots,2m-2
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 1,3,\ldots,2m-1.
\]

Up to nonzero signs and factorials, that coefficient is

\[
 V(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_{2m})
 V(u_1^2,\ldots,u_m^2)^2
 \prod_{\ell=1}^m u_\ell,
 \tag{4.5}
\]

which is nonzero.  Its leading power of `epsilon` is \(m(2m-1)\).

If `k=2m-1`, keep all `m` cosine rows and, after relabelling if necessary, the
first `m-1` sine rows.  The lowest term uses even powers through `2m-2` and
odd powers through `2m-3`; its coefficient is, again up to nonzero constants,

\[
 V(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_{2m-1})
 V(u_1^2,\ldots,u_m^2)
 V(u_1^2,\ldots,u_{m-1}^2)
 \prod_{\ell=1}^{m-1}u_\ell.
 \tag{4.6}
\]

Its leading power is \((m-1)(2m-1)\).  The relevant determinant or maximal
minor is a nonzero analytic function of `epsilon`, so it is nonzero for all
sufficiently small positive `epsilon`.
\(\square\)

#### Proof of Theorem 4.1, sufficiency

Let `z_j(t)=exp(-it log p_j)`.  Equation (2.6) factors as

\[
 g_\beta(t)=P(t)
 \prod_{j=1}^r(1+z_j(t)+\cdots+z_j(t)^{\beta_j-1}),
 \qquad
 P(t)=\prod_{j=1}^r(z_j(t)-1).
 \tag{4.7}
\]

The polynomials

\[
 1,\quad1+z,\quad\ldots,\quad1+z+\cdots+z^{s-2}
\]

are a real unitriangular basis of the polynomials of degree at most `s-2`.
Their tensor products give a real invertible column change from the basis
`h_beta` to the exponentials

\[
 e^{-i\mu_at},
 \qquad
 \mu_a=\sum_{j=1}^ra_j\log p_j,
 \qquad a\in\{0,\ldots,s-2\}^r.
 \tag{4.8}
\]

These `k` frequencies are distinct by unique factorization.  At a
nonresonant time, multiplication by `P(t)` is an invertible two-by-two real
row transformation on the real and imaginary quadratures.  Lemma 4.2 thus
produces one full-rank tuple for the original functions `g_beta`.

Every maximal minor is real analytic in `T`; at least one is not identically
zero.  The rank-deficient schedules are the common zero locus of all maximal
minors and hence lie inside the zero set of this one nonzero minor.  That set
has empty interior and measure zero.  Since a nonzero real-analytic function
cannot vanish throughout any open time window, almost every tuple in every
such window has full rank. \(\square\)

### 4.1 What the threshold does not say

The lower bound applies to the full unbounded real vector space with both
quadratures observed.  Integer, nonnegative, sparse, finite, magnitude-only,
or adaptive models are different.  In particular, an integer lattice can be
exactly identifiable from fewer readings while having zero uniform noise
radius, as shown in Arithmetic Observability I.

## 5. Stable completion by prime-log equidistribution

The preceding theorems say that generic tuples work.  They do not explain
whether a deliberately stable tuple exists.  The arithmetic frequencies give
a stronger constructive answer.

Normalize a trace row by the square root of the ambient dimension:

\[
 \widetilde F_c(t)=s^{-r/2}F_c(t).
 \tag{5.1}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 — arbitrarily near-isometric complex completion

For every `eta>0`, there exist `k=(s-1)^r` distinct times `T` such that every
singular value of

\[
 \widetilde E_T|_{K_{\mathbb C}}:K_{\mathbb C}\to\mathbb C^k
\]

lies in `(1-eta,1+eta)`.

#### Proof

Let \(\zeta=\exp(2\pi i/s)\) and use the orthonormal tensor Fourier basis of
`K`
indexed by

\[
 a\in\{1,\ldots,s-1\}^r.
\]

If a trace row had phase coordinates

\[
 \frac{t\log p_j}{2\pi}\equiv\frac{a_j}{s}\pmod1
 \quad(1\le j\le r),
 \tag{5.2}
\]

then its normalized restriction would be exactly the coordinate functional
at `a`, up to a harmless Fourier sign convention.  All `k` nonconstant phase
grid points would therefore give a unitary restriction.

Use `p_1` as an anchor and set

\[
 t_a=\frac{2\pi}{\log p_1}
 \left(n_a+\frac{a_1}{s}\right).
 \tag{5.3}
\]

The first congruence in (5.2) is exact.  Moreover,

\[
 1,\frac{\log p_2}{\log p_1},\ldots,
 \frac{\log p_r}{\log p_1}
\]

are rationally independent: an integer relation would exponentiate to a
nontrivial equality between products of distinct primes.  Kronecker density
therefore lets us choose an integer `n_a` so that every remaining phase in
(5.2) is as accurate as desired.  Every target neighbourhood is hit
infinitely often by the tail orbit, so the `n_a` may be chosen positive and
pairwise distinct.  The resulting restricted matrix converges entrywise to a
unitary matrix.  Singular values are continuous, so all of them lie in
`(1-eta,1+eta)` for a sufficiently accurate choice.
\(\square\)

This is an existence theorem on an unbounded time axis, not a finite-window
conditioning guarantee.  It also exposes an arithmetic role that survives
the ANOVA reduction: multiplicative independence of the primes makes the
one-dimensional physical orbit dense in the multidimensional Fourier phase
torus.

### 5.1 A certified near-Fourier schedule

The artifact freezes one quantitative instance for `(2,3,5)` and `s=4`.
For every `k=(k_1,k_2,k_3) in {1,2,3}^3`, it uses

\[
 t_k=\frac{2\pi(n_k+k_1/4)}{\log2}.
 \tag{5.4}
\]

The integer arrays `n_k`, with rows indexed by `k_2` and columns by `k_3`,
are

```text
k1=1:  1661137   645383  1111112
        568452  1034181  1348196
       1035255    19501   255511

k1=2:    37928  1833426   587953
       1756495   740741   976751
        663810   977825  1443554

k1=3:   996252  1232262  1775996
       1463055  1699065   683311
        370370   606380  1150114
```

In the orthonormal complex Fourier basis of `K`, the ideal target matrix is
`8 I_27`.  Arb phase enclosures, contraction of orthogonal projection,
`|exp(-ix)-1|<=|x|`, and a Frobenius bound prove

\[
 \lVert B-8I_{27}\rVert_2
 \le \lVert B-8I_{27}\rVert_F
 <\frac{81}{500}.
 \tag{5.5}
\]

Weyl's inequality therefore gives

\[
 \sigma_{\min}(B)>\frac{3919}{500},
 \qquad

 \tag{5.6}
\]

The certified scale-free Gram bound is consequently

\[
 \boxed{\lambda_{\min}((B/8)^*(B/8))>\frac{576}{625}.}
 \tag{5.7}
\]

With the alternative RMS-time normalization `B/sqrt(27)`, the artifact also
proves a Gram bound greater than `9/4`.  This explicit schedule is
deliberately large-time and is not claimed optimal; its role is to turn the

claim concerns the complexified 27-dimensional blind space, not a full raw

## 6. A certified minimal real design for `(2,3,5)`

Take

\[
 (p_1,p_2,p_3)=(2,3,5),\qquad s=4.
\]

Then

\[
 \dim H_{\mathbb R}=64,
 \qquad
 \dim K_{\mathbb R}=3^3=27.
 \tag{6.1}
\]

Theorem 4.1 gives the exact count obstruction

\[
 13\text{ complex readings}\Longrightarrow
 \operatorname{rank}(\mathfrak E_T|_K)\le26<27.
 \tag{6.2}
\]

No choice of thirteen times can complete the full real linear model.

### 6.1 The rational schedule

The certified schedule is the arithmetic progression

\[
 \boxed{
 T_*=\left(\frac{1279}{100},\frac{2\cdot1279}{100},\ldots,
 \frac{14\cdot1279}{100}\right).}
 \tag{6.3}

Thus the times are

 12.79,25.58,38.37,51.16,63.95,76.74,89.53,
 102.32,115.11,127.90,140.69,153.48,166.27,179.06.

They are exact rationals in the certificate; the displayed decimals are not

### 6.2 Restricted interaction geometry

Let `C` be the 64-by-27 integer synthesis matrix whose one-axis columns are

\[
 e_0-e_3,\quad e_1-e_3,\quad e_2-e_3
\]

and whose full columns are all threefold tensor products of those contrasts.
Then

\[
 S_K=C^{\mathsf T}C.

Let `B_*` be the realification of the fourteen raw trace rows restricted by
`C`, and give the completion block its RMS output norm

\[
 \|z\|_{\rm comp}^2=\frac1{14}\sum_{m=1}^{14}|z_m|^2.
 \tag{6.4}

The interval certificate proves the generalized matrix inequality stated in
the pinned artifact:

\[
 \boxed{\frac1{14}B_*^{\mathsf T}B_*\succ
 \frac1{25}S_K.}
 \tag{6.5}

Thus the intrinsic restricted singular value is greater than `1/5`.  The
fourteen readings are not merely full-rank: they define an explicit

\[
 \lambda_{\min}

 \approx0.04822143415309486.

### 6.3 Complete raw acquisition geometry

The full operator uses:

- 48 resonant trace readouts, namely 16 for each of the three prime axes; and
- the 14 nonresonant traces in (6.3).

The three resonant blocks each include `t=0`.  Thus this accounting describes
62 acquisition rows, including three replicated zero-time measurements; no
claim about the number of distinct time locations is needed.

No inverse Vandermonde matrix is applied.  Let `A_*` be the unscaled 62-by-64
complex trace matrix and define

\[
 G_{\rm raw,*}:=\frac1{62}\Re(A_*^*A_*).
\]

Each of the 62 raw complex trace
readouts is retained, realified isometrically, and given the same adversarial
RMS weight:

\[
 \|y\|_{\rm raw,RMS}^2
 =\frac1{62}\sum_{\ell=1}^{62}|y_\ell|^2.
 \tag{6.7}
\]

For the 64-dimensional real source with its ordinary Euclidean norm, the
certificate proves

\[
 \boxed{G_{\rm raw,*}\succ\frac1{500}I_{64}.}
 \tag{6.8}
\]

The formal result implies a full raw singular value greater than
`1/sqrt(500)`.  A separate binary64 reconstruction gives the descriptive
values

\[
 \lambda_{\min}(G_{\rm raw,*})
 \approx0.007310355598523784,
 \qquad
 \sigma_{\min}\approx0.0855006175329967.
 \tag{6.9}
\]

This closes the calibration gap left open in Arithmetic Observability I: the
stability statement is carried by the actual raw harmonic acquisition, under
the explicitly declared equal-weight RMS norm.

### 6.4 Exact minimax interpretation

For any real linear acquisition `A`, source metric `S>0`, and output metric
`W>0`, define

\[
 \Lambda(A;S,W)=
 \inf_{c\ne0}
 \frac{(Ac)^{\mathsf T}W(Ac)}{c^{\mathsf T}Sc}.
 \tag{6.10}
\]

If \(\Lambda>0\), then for the unbounded source model and adversarial noise
\(\lVert\eta\rVert_W\le\varepsilon\), the exact minimax radius is

\[
 \boxed{
 \inf_{\widehat c}\sup_{c,\|\eta\|_W\le\varepsilon}
 \|\widehat c(Ac+\eta)-c\|_S
 =\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{\Lambda(A;S,W)}}.}
 \tag{6.11}
\]

The whitened Moore--Penrose inverse gives the upper bound.  For the lower
bound, take an `S`-unit least-observable direction `h`; the datum zero is
compatible with

\[
 c_\pm=\pm\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt\Lambda}h
\]

using opposite admissible noises.  Their source distance is
\(2\varepsilon/\sqrt\Lambda\), so every decoder makes error at least half
that distance on one alternative.

For the restricted completion block, (6.5) gives the certified upper minimax
radius `5 epsilon`.  For the complete raw operator, (6.8) gives the certified
upper radius `sqrt(500) epsilon`.  These are conservative consequences, not
claims that either rational floor is spectrally sharp.

Equations (6.2) and (6.11) are matching statements.  Below fourteen readings,
the stability floor is exactly zero for every schedule.  At fourteen, the
explicit schedule has a formally positive floor.

## 7. What geometry has been created?

Before completion, the resonant pseudometric identifies every affine set

\[
 c+K_{\mathbb R}
\]

to a single observational point.  The top interaction has zero length in
every direction.  After adding `T_*`, the raw Gram `G_raw,*` defines

\[
 d_*(c,c')^2=(c-c')^{\mathsf T}G_{\rm raw,*}(c-c').
 \tag{7.1}
\]

The certified positive floor makes this a genuine metric on the entire
64-dimensional real model.  On `K`, the restriction is an explicit
27-dimensional ellipsoid.  Its least observational stretch is bounded away
from zero by the interval certificate.  The observational quotient has
therefore been
lifted into a resolved metric space, rather than merely assigned a new label.

This is the precise sense in which acquisition creates a distinguishability
geometry.  The geometry is not claimed to pre-exist independently of the
protocol: it is induced jointly by the arithmetic trace, the allowed source
class, the chosen times, and the noise norm.

## 8. Failure modes and falsifiers

The theorem and certificate expose several ways the conclusion can fail.

1. **Too few quadratures.**  With thirteen complex readings, a real null
   direction is unavoidable.  With only real parts, the general threshold
   reverts to at least 27 readings.
2. **Complex source coefficients.**  Fourteen readings do not complete the
   full complex space.  The exact complex threshold is 27.
3. **Bad timing.**  Repeated times, resonant rows, or schedules approaching
   them can have zero or arbitrarily small floor even at the threshold count.
4. **Unknown tails.**  An unrestricted out-of-box tail can span the sample
   space and destroy prefix observability.  The finite prime box is a real
   assumption, not notation.
5. **Clock jitter.**  On an unbounded coefficient space, timing error is not a
   uniformly bounded additive perturbation.  A jitter theorem needs an
   amplitude or source-norm bound.
6. **Noise metric substitution.**  A calibrated marginal floor cannot be
   quoted as an equal-weight raw-sample floor.  The full certificate avoids that
   substitution by reconstructing the raw Gram directly.
7. **Arithmetic constraints.**  Positivity, integrality, sparsity, or an
   actual number-field family can lower the exact identification count, but
   such a result would not automatically inherit the linear minimax radius.

Any independent interval reconstruction that finds a nonpositive leading
minor for either declared shifted Gram would falsify the corresponding
certificate claim.  Any exact 13-reading injective real-linear construction
would falsify Theorem 4.1, but rank--nullity rules it out.

## 9. Formal certificate architecture

The verifier reconstructs rather than trusts the mathematical payload.

1. It validates exact JSON types, fixed dimensions, distinct prime axes,
   rational times, declared normalizations, and resource limits.
2. It builds the integer contrast synthesis and its exact source metric.
3. It evaluates `log p`, `pi`, sine, cosine, and every phase using Arb balls at
   the pinned precision.
4. Independently of the stored evidence, it reconstructs the restricted real
   Gram and the complete raw 64-by-64 Gram from the defining phase formulas.
5. For a claimed rational lower `L`, it forms `G-LS` and certifies positive
   definiteness through strictly positive interval enclosures of every
   leading principal minor.
6. It compares the reconstructed payload using canonical JSON bytes and pins
   the artifact digest.

The determinant signs and their outward interval enclosures are evidence,
not assumed inputs.  Binary64 values may be included as descriptive discovery
diagnostics, but they do not decide verification.

## 12. Provenance and novelty boundary

The prime-resonant marginal theorem, calibrated ANOVA spectrum, exact
27-dimensional kernel, and object-first observability framework are inherited
from Arithmetic Observability I.  Kronecker density, Vandermonde arguments,
analytic zero-set facts, tensor Fourier bases, generalized eigenvalues, and
Moore--Penrose minimax recovery are standard ingredients.

Tensor-product ANOVA places the common marginal kernel in the classical
highest-order zero-sum interaction subspace
([Takemura, 1983](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1983.10477037)).  After the
real triangular basis change in (4.7)--(4.8), the completion matrices belong
to the generalized exponential-Vandermonde and unisolvent sampling setting;
nearby work includes random Vandermonde sampling
([Bass--Grochenig](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0036141003432316)), full-spark
one-parameter orbit frames
([Maniscalco--Oussa](https://doi.org/10.1080/03081087.2020.1822272)), and
multivariate exponential reconstruction
([Kunis et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2015.10.023)).  The prime-log
trajectory is the finite-prime Bohr--Kronecker flow classical in Dirichlet
series theory
([Hedenmalm--Lindqvist--Seip](https://doi.org/10.1215/S0012-7094-97-08601-4));
quantitative hitting-time limitations are part of localized Kronecker
approximation
([Gonek--Montgomery](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.indag.2016.02.002)).  These
precedents motivate the terminology and sharply limit the novelty claim.

The contribution asserted within this research programme is their synthesis
into:

1. field-sensitive exact acquisition thresholds for completing the prime-box
   interaction;
2. a paired-row proof that real harmonic traces attain the dimension lower
   bound generically;
3. a prime-log torus construction showing arbitrarily near-isometric complex
   completion; and
4. a reproducible raw-trace interval certificate at the sharp real sample
   count.

No external priority claim is made.  A focused literature search did not find
the paired-quadrature threshold for this prime-box curve stated verbatim, but
that absence is not evidence of priority; broader review and independent

## 13. Questions opened by the completion theorem

The next target should be selected by information gain, not by numbering.

1. **Bounded-window optimum.**  Determine the best possible raw floor for 14
   times in a prescribed interval, with a matching dual upper certificate.
2. **Jitter geometry.**  Add a bounded source norm and derive the exact joint
   noise--clock stability modulus.
3. **Arithmetic model reduction.**  Replace the full real box by nonnegative
   integer arrays or actual Euler-factor families and determine whether fewer
   traces give stable, rather than merely exact, recovery.
4. **Tail-compatible completion.**  Combine the finite-box completion with
   divisor-dominated compact tail fibres and certify inter-fibre separation.
5. **Adaptive completion.**  Determine whether nonlinear or adaptive timing
   can improve stability at the same worst-case reading count.
6. **Intrinsic geometry.**  Compare the raw distinguishability ellipsoid with
   algebraic distances between local Euler data, rather than treating every
   coefficient direction equally.

The decisive result here is modest but sharp: an exact 27-dimensional
arithmetic blind spot is neither permanent nor free to remove.  Its price is
27 complex-linear readings, or 14 complex readings when the arithmetic source
is real.  At that minimum price, a concrete raw harmonic protocol already
creates a certified positive geometry.
