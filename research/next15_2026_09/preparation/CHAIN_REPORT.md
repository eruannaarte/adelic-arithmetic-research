# Three adaptive cycles in nonlinear preparation robustness

This chain improves the double-pendulum research from a small isotropic preparation allowance to a much larger, explicitly allocated calibration region. It preserves the original mechanical model, local sensor access, launch charges, finite source box, shared clock, and deterministic observation-noise metric. The contribution is a sequence of mathematical theorems and outward numerical certificates. There are no hardware measurements and no claim of experimental feasibility or literature novelty.

The starting package in `research/five_paths_2026_09/path1_preparation/` certified unknown preparation errors of at most 10^-7 in each initial angle and each scaled angular velocity. The source consists of two logarithmic mechanical parameters and one shared finite clock parameter, each in [-10^-4,10^-4]. Every reading from a launch shares its unknown preparation vector. Budget shares determine the existing weighted noise metric, whose radius remains 10^-7. This is bounded nuisance uncertainty, not an observed preparation input or a collection of extra source variables fitted from the same readings.

The first cycle asked whether the earlier preparation restriction reflected lost structure in the proof. The baseline propagated an absolute bias for each output before decoding. That discarded the fact that one initial error produces signed, related changes in several readings from the same launch. The replacement integrates all four launch-coordinate sensitivity columns together with the two physical-source columns in a 31-coordinate augmented ODE. One finite clock is retained through the exact endpoint time-dilation derivative. Outward Picard–Taylor tubes enclose the whole parameter/preparation box, not just nominal derivatives.

The new theorem multiplies the preparation sensitivity matrix by a fixed decoder before taking absolute values. Integrating along preparation and source segments then bounds the diameter of every feasible source set under bounded noise. This preserves useful cancellation while still permitting every preparation vector in the declared box. An exact two-reading control distinguishes shared preparation, which can cancel, from separately prepared readings, for which that cancellation is invalid.

The preregistered preparation radius 10^-6 passed. With selected A+B budget shares, the certified source diameter is below 1.640686e-5, compared with about 2.011628e-5 in the baseline at one tenth that preparation radius. Uniform A+B gives 1.600212e-5, and uniform A+B+C gives 2.112889e-5. Using the same new sensitivity intervals but discarding signs before projection gives bounds around 2.96e-5 to 3.56e-5. Thus the gain has a demonstrated structural source. It is a comparison of sufficient bounds, not a claim that actual minimax errors have been ordered.

Uniform A+B performing slightly better than the historical selection suggested the second question: should the decoder itself target finite preparation robustness? A new convex synthesis objective combines finite source-Jacobian deviation, the signed preparation map, and weighted noise amplification. A linear epigraph program generates candidates. Each candidate is converted to an exact rational left inverse, and its actual finite bound is recomputed independently. Neither numerical optimizer success nor approximate equality constraints carry proof authority.

The ambitious primary test, preparation radius 10^-5 with target source diameter 10^-4, failed for all three synthesized candidates. That failure is retained. Their bounds improved over least squares, but none met the target. The subsequent revision kept the same large-box enclosures and already fixed decoders, then tested a preparation subbox of radius 7*10^-6. A row-specific finite inverse criterion certified this revised box for all three designs. All original least-squares decoders failed the identical sufficient criterion. Exact calibration boundaries range from about 7.6644e-6 to 7.6835e-6 for the synthesized decoders, versus 5.0035e-6 to 6.5970e-6 for their controls. This establishes a decoder/calibration tradeoff without changing sensing resources.

The remaining gap to 10^-5 motivated the third cycle. Equal precision in eight different launch coordinates may be unnecessarily restrictive. For selected A+B and uniform A+B, the same five sensors and group charges were retained while the eight preparation radii were allowed to differ, each bounded by the already validated outer radius. The finite inverse theorem defines a polytope of admissible calibration radii. The research objective became maximizing its box volume.

This cycle adds a global certificate, not just another numerical optimum. A rational feasible box supplies a lower bound on volume. Nonnegative rational multipliers combine the calibration constraints, and an elementary arithmetic–geometric mean argument supplies an upper bound valid for every feasible box in that polytope. The exact ratio between these bounds establishes near-optimality for the fixed decoder certificate. It does not establish optimality over every possible decoder or over the true nonlinear inverse problem.

The result concentrates the tighter requirement in launch B's first angle. Selected A+B permits seven radii at 10^-5 and B.theta1 at 6.270072214e-6. Uniform A+B permits B.theta1 at 6.300536639e-6, with the other seven at the cap up to downward rounding. The geometric mean radii are about 9.43e-6, and their volumes exceed their respective best isotropic certificate volumes by factors of 5.26 and 5.18. Exact primal/dual ratios exceed 0.99999999998 and 0.99999999946.

Across the chain, fresh nonlinear replays, an independent mechanical-equation implementation, derivative diagnostics, exact rational checks, and adverse/boundary tests support the stated results. The fast verifier is conditional on the supplied ODE enclosures; the deep mode rebuilds all three enclosures from source. Arb arithmetic and the analytic enclosure proof remain trusted components. The next practical question is whether instrumentation can establish these joint launch-accuracy bounds, especially for B.theta1. That question is explicitly open, and no fourth cycle is counted here.
