# Three adaptive cycles on harmonic acquisition

This chain changes the assessment of short harmonic schedules in two directions. It proves a substantially stronger universal acquisition-time obstruction, then shows why a seemingly successful short finite-source design can still fail completely on actual product distributions. All three cycles use the original finite model: three labelled probability factors on exponents 0 through 3, primes 2,3,5, six fixed complex harmonic readings, Euclidean factor distance, and raw Euclidean observation norm. The historical source files and previous certificates remain unchanged.

The first question was whether the old single-factor cubic obstruction missed arithmetic structure. At the previously certified target factor floor .815, that bound required maximum time only 1.64705, much shorter than the existing sufficient schedules. Product vertices supply another source of difficulty: the two integer labels 24 and 25 correspond to legal exponent vectors(3,1,0) and(0,0,2). Their factor distance is sqrt 6, but their harmonic phases differ only at frequency log(25/24). Consequently their response difference remains small over a short observation interval.

Cycle 1 made this into a complete universal theorem. A vertex pair changing k factors and having logarithmic frequency gap delta bounds every m-reading schedule's floor by sqrt(2m/k) times sin(min(T delta,pi)/2). The strongest member of this single-pair family can be found exactly: enumerate exponent differences, classify them by k, and compare their positive integer ratios using rational arithmetic. All 171 oriented differences are covered. The minimum ratios are 2,27/25,25/24 for one,two,three changed factors. An independent enumeration of all 2016 pairs among 64 actual vertices confirms the classification.

For six readings and floor .815, the new necessary horizon is 20.56316841858..., over 12.4848 times the previous cubic bound. In particular, every schedule with all absolute times at most 20 fails that target. This is a meaningful acquisition obstruction, but it remains a necessary condition. A resonant six-reading schedule provides the negative control: it can satisfy the horizon requirement while being exactly blind to one factor.

That limitation determined the second objective. The first theorem maximized one pair at a time, although different pairs may demand incompatible observation times. Cycle 2 therefore considered simultaneous vertex constraints. Each normalized squared pair distance is a sum of functions f_v(t)=(1-cos(delta_v t))/k_v. A convex combination of these functions gives an upper bound on the minimum pair distance. Bounding that combination over the complete time interval gives a universal design obstruction without needing a numerical linear-program optimum.

A two-pair witness using ratios 25/24 and 27/20, with exact weights 1761/2000 and 239/2000, rules out every six-reading schedule with absolute times at most 21.8 at the same floor .815. Its supremum is certified by outward evaluation of 4096 adjoining time intervals, not by checking selected time points. The floor upper is below .81277.

The more informative outcome of Cycle 2 was a positive certificate for the finite vertex problem. Six exact times between 15.790052 and 29.966619 separate every one of the 64 product vertices with factor floor greater than 1.03. All 171 distinct exponent-difference constraints are verified with outward arithmetic, and a separate raw-product implementation checks all 2016 vertex pairs. Thus a method restricted to these vertices cannot rule out a horizon below 30 at the desired .815 floor. This did not prove full-source recovery; it isolated the precise gap the next cycle had to examine.

Cycle 3 consequently moved into the interior of the product simplices, using exactly that six-time schedule. It found two normalized product distributions about .8 apart whose floating responses were nearly equal. A small numerical residual would have been insufficient: it might reflect conditioning, a nearby diagonal solution, or finite precision. The next step therefore froze exact rational source-center coordinates, held six coordinates fixed, and treated the twelve real response-difference equations as a square system in twelve active coordinates.

The resulting interval proof establishes an exact collision. On a rational coordinate box of radius 1e-9, the map x-Rf(x), with a fixed rational approximate inverse R, has derivative norm at most 2.922557e-6 and maps the entire box strictly into itself. The supplied proof constructs its unique fixed point by a convergent geometric iteration and shows R is invertible, so the fixed point is an actual zero of the original response equations. Every one of the 24 factor probabilities is greater than .07 throughout the relevant source box, and the factor distance is greater than .79. The equality therefore occurs between distinct strictly interior sources, under the original acquisition model.

This is stronger than a poor conditioning example. The short schedule's full-product global floor is exactly zero while its vertex floor exceeds 1.03. The finite vertex relaxation can therefore give a convincing but false impression of continuous-source recoverability. The result does not say that every schedule below 30 fails. It proves failure for this explicit, fully certified vertex design and explains why merely adding more vertex checks cannot fix the logical problem.

Every cycle has a complete proof or source-connected outward certificate, a worked witness, and independent or negative controls. All 13 accumulated tests pass, including 256/384-bit reconstruction, separate integer-label tensor expansion, affine-coordinate derivative identities, and false-claim/domain rejection. Primary literature places frequency-gap obstructions and validated nonlinear zeros within established superresolution and numerical-verification methods; no historical-priority claim is made. The next justified question is to combine short-time search with a valid certificate over all factor-source chords, or turn the interior collision into an obstruction uniform over an entire schedule region. That successor is recorded but not counted as a fourth cycle.
