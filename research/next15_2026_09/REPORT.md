# Fifteen adaptive research cycles: findings and conclusions

The continuation produced fifteen completed developments across the five research paths: new exact obstructions, a sharp arithmetic threshold, and stronger certified recovery procedures. The largest practical mathematical gain is a **certified multiscale weighted sensor-noise threshold more than 127 times the original** through a computable correction. The strongest caution is an explicit harmonic schedule that distinguishes every product vertex yet maps two distinct interior sources to exactly the same observations.

These are five linked sequences of three cycles each, with overlapping work across agents. Within each sequence, the preceding result determined the next question. The numbering below is reading order, not a claim that fifteen experiments ran in one chronological chain. Each counted cycle supplies a complete argument, a numerical certificate connected to its model, or a proved obstruction. Tests, audits, failed pilots, and document preparation are not counted as cycles. The [cycle ledger](/Volumes/KINGSTON/Vibecoding/Math/research/next15_2026_09/CYCLE_LEDGER.md) records each question, result, limitation, and successor; the [evidence index](/Volumes/KINGSTON/Vibecoding/Math/research/next15_2026_09/EVIDENCE_INDEX.md) links every claim to its proof and reconstruction.

## Harmonic acquisition: vertex success can conceal exact failure

The model has three labelled probability factors on exponents 0 through 3, associated with primes 2, 3, and 5. It uses six fixed complex harmonic readings, Euclidean factor distance, and the raw Euclidean observation norm. The target global lower recovery factor is 0.815.

Cycle 1 uses two legal product vertices with integer labels 24 and 25. Their factor distance is √6, but their phase separation grows only at frequency log(25/24). An exact classification of every exponent difference proves a necessary horizon of **20.56316841858**, over 12.48 times the previous cubic obstruction. This is a necessary time bound; a long enough schedule can still be blind.

Cycle 2 accounts for the fact that different source pairs may prefer incompatible times. A convex combination of two pair constraints, bounded over the entire continuous time interval with outward arithmetic, excludes every schedule whose absolute times are at most **21.8**. A separate positive construction has six rational times at most 29.966619 and distinguishes all 64 product vertices with lower factor greater than **1.03**.

That positive result exposed the next question: do the same readings distinguish distributions between the vertices? Cycle 3 proves they do not. A rational interval box and a contraction argument establish two sources with identical readings, all 24 factor probabilities greater than 0.07, and factor distance greater than 0.79. This is an existence proof of an exact collision, rather than an interpretation of a small floating residual.

The schedule therefore has vertex factor greater than 1.03 and full-product global factor **zero**. This does not rule out every short schedule. It establishes that finite vertex separation is insufficient to certify continuous-source identification, even in this small explicit model. Short-time design must control all source differences. ## Preparation: shared uncertainty creates exploitable structure

The double-pendulum work preserves the mechanical model, local reading options, and launch costs. Its two logarithmic mechanical parameters and shared clock parameter each lie in [−10⁻⁴,10⁻⁴]. Each launch has four bounded, unknown preparation coordinates shared by its readings. The noise radius is 10⁻⁷ in the existing budget-weighted norm; changing budget shares changes that norm's ball.

Cycle 4 propagates the complete preparation sensitivity and applies the fixed decoder before taking absolute values. This retains cancellations produced by one preparation error affecting several readings. Fresh outward nonlinear ODE integration validates the entire parameter and preparation boxes. At preparation radius **10⁻⁶**, ten times the baseline, the selected five-reading design has certified source diameter below **1.640686×10⁻⁵**, compared with the previous bound of about 2.011628×10⁻⁵ at radius 10⁻⁷. These are improved sufficient bounds, not measured minimax errors.

Cycle 5 changes the decoder to account for preparation uncertainty. Numerical optimization proposes candidates; exact rational left inverses and finite inequalities decide validity. The initial radius 10⁻⁵ fails the target diameter 10⁻⁴ and remains recorded as a failure. Keeping the already fixed decoder and larger-box enclosures, radius **7×10⁻⁶** passes for all three design comparisons. Their original least-squares controls fail the same sufficient gate. Failure of that gate is not an impossibility theorem.

Cycle 6 asks whether every coordinate needs the same tolerance. For the two five-reading designs, seven radii remain at or just below 10⁻⁵ while launch B's first angle receives the tighter radius of about **6.27×10⁻⁶** or **6.30×10⁻⁶**. The resulting certified box volumes exceed their respective best isotropic certificate volumes by factors greater than **5.26** and **5.18**. Rational feasible witnesses and an arithmetic–geometric mean dual bound prove the volumes are within 6×10⁻¹⁰ relative of the optimum of the fixed certificate polytope.

The useful output is a concrete allocation of calibration accuracy, with globally certified near-optimality within the fixed certificate polytope. It does not prove an optimal physical experiment or establish that an apparatus meets these tolerances. ## Quadratic fields: a sharp limit, then a conditional gain

Cycles 7 and 8 study the ideal coefficient norm of the sequence a(n)/n² for actual real quadratic fields, with query a(5)∈{0,1,2}. Cycle 9 returns to the finite zeta-reading system and its weighted sensor norm. These metrics must remain distinct.

For the first 50 coefficients, set

\[
J=5^{-4}+20^{-4}+45^{-4}.
\]

Cycle 7 proves that the exact minimum squared distances for the label pairs (0,1), (1,2), and (0,2) are respectively

\[
J,\qquad J+4\,25^{-4},\qquad 4J+4\,25^{-4}.
\]

The proof combines multiplicative identities with nonnegative squared coordinate differences. Genuine fields attain all three minima. Three explicit fundamental discriminants have checked coefficient prefixes and recursive integer primality certificates. General realization uses finite prime prescriptions, the Chinese remainder theorem, and Dirichlet's theorem with coprime progressions; the proof checks their hypotheses.

The sharp universal ideal prefix-noise radius has boundary √J/2≈**0.02004054555934**. At the boundary, two different labels can share a noisy observation, because the minimum is attained.

Cycle 8 determines the full infinite-sequence infimum:

\[
\inf_{a(5)\ne b(5)}\left\|\frac{a(n)-b(n)}{n^2}\right\|_{\ell^2}
=\frac{\sqrt{\zeta(8)}}{25}.
\]

No pair of actual fields attains this value. Forced parity at indices 5^(2h+1)m² gives the lower bound; an additional split prime gives strictness for actual fields. Finite prime prescriptions and a complete tail bound construct arbitrarily close approximants. No field with infinitely prescribed inert primes is assumed.

Consequently, label uniqueness under arbitrary closed-ball noise holds **including** radius √ζ(8)/50≈**0.02004073208441**, and fails above it. This is uniqueness on promised infinite observations, not an efficient infinite-data algorithm. The full sequence improves the ideal worst-case radius over the first 50 coefficients by only about **0.000931%**. The endpoint distinction is more substantial than the numerical gain: closure can change whether equality is recoverable.

That tiny universal improvement motivated Cycle 9 to exploit conditional information. After recovering a(2) and a(3), seven coefficients at 5, 10, 15, 20, 30, 40, and 45 are known multiples of a(5). A rational weighted query uses all seven while retaining the complete finite and remote tail bounds.

All nine small-prime strata certify on the actual **8,900-reading** system. Declared weighted-noise radii range from **0.02003** when 2 and 3 are inert to **0.02324** when both split. In the split/split case, the sufficient boundary improves by **more than 14.21%** over the original three-coordinate query in that same stratum and metric. The all-inert theoretical boundary is unchanged; its slightly larger declared decimal radius is not a structural gain. [Arithmetic proofs, accepted inputs, and sensor connection](/Volumes/KINGSTON/Vibecoding/Math/research/next15_2026_09/quadratic/PROOFS.md).

## Arithmetic noise: preprocessing yields the largest improvement

The source class consists of integer coefficients 0≤a(n)≤d₁₄(n), with 50 retained coefficients and 8,900 distinct readings. The 2,550 inner-window readings are reused outer readings, so their noise cannot be treated as independent extra data.

Cycle 10 proves a limitation over the entire original inner/outer mixture family. Its complete absolute-tail/Neumann certificate can succeed only for an inner weight between 0.0017613 and 0.0020428. Throughout that interval, the actual coefficient-50 iid variance ratio to the outer-only design exceeds **1.00009090067766**. The exclusion is continuous, with the reused-reading covariance included. It limits this sufficient certification method, not the information contained in every excluded design.

Cycle 11 changes preprocessing. Because every omitted coefficient lies in a known one-sided interval, subtract its complete midpoint response:

\[
c(t)=\frac12\left[\zeta(2+it)^{14}
-\sum_{n\le50}d_{14}(n)n^{-2-it}\right].
\]

Every remaining omitted coefficient has half its original absolute bound. Thus the complete tail-bias bound halves while the decoder's sensor-noise covariance is unchanged. All 8,900 correction entries are supplied as rational numbers, with outward special-function bounds and approximation error below 10⁻²⁰ per complex reading.

All 50 integers now certify at weighted sensor radius **10⁻⁴** for the reference mixture and **8×10⁻⁵** for the outer-only design. The reference sufficient threshold exceeds 0.000100356572843797, **more than 127.237 times** the original threshold. A common coordinatewise error bound of 8×10⁻⁵ suffices for both designs. No extra source readings are required, and no sharp minimax claim is made.

Cycle 12 uses this margin to remove an arbitrary complex affine baseline β₀+β₁t. It requires the known normalization **a(1)=1**: without it, changing a(1) and the constant baseline creates an exact collision. An augmented decoder with a certified Schur complement charges a complete new tail channel. It recovers the 49 unknown integers, supplies the known first coefficient, and tolerates radii **9×10⁻⁵** and **7×10⁻⁵** in the respective weighted norms.

This is the strongest application direction in the continuation: a computable use of known source bounds produces a large robustness margin, then supports explicit nuisance removal. Arbitrarily large affine drift is covered by the exact mathematical solve; finite-precision implementations need a separate error budget. ## Resolution: location can be recovered before the source

The declared Neumann model keeps a two-dimensional source space, n=1001, coupling g=4/5, and known time τ∈[1,2]. Its three source calibrations—L², continuum H¹, and natural discrete H¹—remain separate.

Cycle 13 lifts the information matrix to a graph-semigroup calculation. Interior translated closed walks agree until they reach a boundary and return; an explicit exponential-tail bound handles all later walks. Every target from **41 through 959**, a total of 919 positions, retains **more than 99%** of the previous central finite information floors throughout the time interval. The bound is uniform rather than a sampled target sweep.

Cycle 14 treats target location as unknown. Reflection symmetry makes the infinite-lattice energy centroid equal the target, even for cancelling source combinations. A finite-boundary first-moment estimate and a deterministic noise inequality show that relative output noise **10⁻⁴** permits exact integer localization for targets 47 through 953: total centroid error is below 0.279067 cells. The correctly selected least-squares inverse then recovers the source with separately calibrated error bounds. A zero source cannot identify location, and arbitrarily small sources cannot tolerate a fixed positive absolute noise floor.

Cycle 15 adds the prior that the target lies within ten cells of 500. The **99 spatial channels from 451 through 549** retain more than 99% of the original central information floors. Relative window-output noise **10⁻³** still gives exact target localization, with centroid error below 0.198181 cells. This is a changed observation operator and a narrower prior, not a like-for-like universal tenfold noise improvement.

Off-center targets activate modes absent at exact center. Each spatial channel still uses a global average in the source coordinate. Direct acquisition savings require access to those spatial readouts; forming the window after acquiring all modal data gives only a processing reduction. Nor is 99 proved minimal. ## What the results justify

The most useful common lesson is that measurement count alone does not determine recoverability. Source geometry, correlations in uncertainty, prior bounds, and the precise quantity being queried can change the answer. Vertex testing missed an exact collision; preserving shared preparation effects enlarged a certified region; envelope centering created a large noise margin; and conditional arithmetic information improved a query whose universal ideal margin was already nearly exhausted.

The next objectives are now concrete: certify short harmonic schedules over all source differences; measure the joint launch tolerances needed by the preparation certificate; add uncertain a(7) and the nonlinear a(25) relation to an actual-sensor query; extend baseline removal to higher polynomial drift with complete tail channels; and certify a smaller asymmetric spatial sensing bank. These are proposed successors, outside the fifteen completed cycles.

All five chains have separate audits, complete proofs, source-connected certificates, and adverse controls. The consolidated [reproduction guide](/Volumes/KINGSTON/Vibecoding/Math/research/next15_2026_09/README.md) distinguishes newly reconstructed computations from inherited verified premises. The final verification runs all cycle checkers, all **66 focused tests**, and fresh nonlinear preparation replays. The complete quadratic sensor tail was also freshly reproduced; unchanged degree-14 baseline tails and central resolution premises retain their documented earlier reconstructions. These checks support the stated mathematical guarantees under the declared models. They do not establish physical performance, proof-assistant verification, or historical priority.
