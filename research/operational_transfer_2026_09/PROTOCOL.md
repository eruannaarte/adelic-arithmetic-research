# Operational consequences of the transfer theorem

Started 2026-09-05 in response to the request to pursue both selected next targets.

The existing transfer theorem is the common mathematical foundation. This phase
develops two applications without rewriting the historical research packages.

1. **Restricted spatial sensing.** Reduce the certified eleven-row bank, aiming
   toward the necessary four-row benchmark. Require all 21 targets, all 210
   target pairs, every known real time in [1,2], and every nonzero two-mode
   source. Report a noise/accuracy tradeoff in the original output and source
   metrics. A sampled search alone is not an accepted certificate; failure of a
   sufficient bound is not impossibility or a minimum-row theorem.
2. **Arithmetic sensing with bounded residual drift.** Implement a verifier that
   actually evaluates the exact-model normal residual from supplied readings and
   a proposed augmented solution. Combine the data-dependent solve error with
   complete arithmetic tails, sensor noise, the digital correction error, and
   an explicitly bounded nonpolynomial remainder. Demonstrate recovery and safe
   rejection on reproducible synthetic data. A small normal residual establishes
   solve accuracy, not the truth of an assumed physical drift/noise budget.

Acceptance requires complete arguments, executable consequence checks, outward
numerical reconstruction where used, meaningful adverse controls, and a concise
report stating remaining assumptions. Reuse hash-bound historical premises and
their documented reconstruction commands rather than silently replacing them.
Preserve previous packages byte-for-byte. Only the current-state index is mutable.

This is a bounded extension of the two requested paths. It does not claim a
minimum spatial bank, a measured apparatus calibration, statistical optimality,
or historical novelty of the general transfer principle.
