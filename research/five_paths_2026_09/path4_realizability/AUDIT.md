# Independent Path 4 audit

The finite-prefix construction, complete-tail decomposition, and constrained-query arithmetic pass independent review for **all real quadratic fields**, without a discriminant ceiling. The certified query is `a(5)` at adversarial noise radius `2001/100000 = .02001` in the declared weighted probability-measure norm. This audit is not a claim of literature novelty or a formal proof-assistant verification.

## Mathematical checks

**Realizability.** Reconstructed the CRT choices in `quadratic.py`: the ramified product `R` is squarefree; every odd-prime residue imposed on the auxiliary prime is nonzero; the modulo-eight residue is odd; and all CRT moduli are coprime. For nonramified odd `p`, the chosen Legendre sign of the auxiliary prime cancels the sign of `R`. For ramified `p`, choosing a nonzero auxiliary residue avoids square factors. The two-adic branch gives the prescribed character or ramification. A prime in the resulting coprime residue class, larger than every specified prime, therefore produces a positive squarefree `d=Rq` and the desired fundamental discriminant. Dirichlet's theorem supplies infinitely many such primes, hence distinct real quadratic fields. It supplies no discriminant ceiling or efficient bound on the search. The multiplicative prime-power coefficient rule is necessary and sufficient for the finite prefix; a divisor-envelope vector alone is insufficient.

**Infinite tail.** With `D(x)=sum_(n<=x)d_2(n) <= x(1+log x)`, partial summation gives `sum_(n>x)d_2(n)/n^2 <= 2(log x+2)/x`. Dropping the negative endpoint term makes this an upper bound. Every omitted index is covered: Arb enumerates `51..10000`; the alias-free response bound covers `10001..10^9`; and positivity plus exact normalization gives the unit response cap beyond `10^9`. The middle bound multiplies the entire mass above `10000`, so its overlap with the final mass term is conservative. The common-spacing identity is `T/(2m)=1/10`; all eight shifted denominator arguments lie between the certified positive endpoints inside `(0,pi)`. Concavity of sine proves the minimum occurs at an endpoint. The mixture is combined with its correct signed response before taking absolute values in the finite-tail and Gram calculations.

**Constrained query.** On recovering `a(2),a(3)`, the values `a(4),a(9)` are known and each belongs to `{1,3}`. Coprime multiplicativity gives `a(20)=a(4)a(5)` and `a(45)=a(9)a(5)`. For `J=sum c_n^2/n^4` and `alpha_n=(c_n/n^4)/J`, the estimator has exact coefficient normalization `sum alpha_n c_n=1`. Its noise dual vector is `v_n=alpha_n n^2`, with `||v||^2=1/J`; the Gram floor `1-q` therefore gives noise amplification at most `1/sqrt((1-q)J)`. The positive weighted bias sum and the two anchor bounds are correct. Reusing the same arbitrary noise vector in both stages requires no independence assumption because every stage is guaranteed for that vector.

The worst of the four arithmetic cases has radius approximately `.02003156766635`; `.02001` therefore passes strictly. The independent-prefix pair differing only at index five has response distance `1/25` in the same norm and overlapping noise balls at `.02`. That pair belongs to the coefficient envelope, not the field family. The comparison correctly demonstrates a constrained-class improvement rather than a field-realizable impossibility witness.

These tests complement the proof and outward arithmetic; they do not replace either.

## Limits

The result concerns one finite arithmetic query with known exact sample times and adversarial weighted-norm noise. It is not recovery of the entire number field, all coefficients, unknown gain/clock recovery, a discriminant bound, or an optimal-noise theorem. The improvement over `.02` is small but strictly certified. Finite-prefix freedom itself implies that the same admissible prefix can come from infinitely many distinct fields. The remaining trusted dependencies for a full run are the accepted arithmetic theorems, the analytic derivation, Python exact arithmetic, and Arb for the finite-tail and Gram reconstruction.
