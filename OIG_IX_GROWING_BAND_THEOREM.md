# Operational Information Geometry IX

## A growing-band response-Gramian theorem

**Status:** analytic theorem memo; not peer reviewed

**Model:** the cell-centred Neumann path and its continuum limit

**Purpose:** replace one fixed source port by a finite or Sobolev-calibrated
source family, and determine when the smallest nonzero response singular value
survives the resolved, finite-cell lattice, and atomic charts

---

## 1. Result in one paragraph

The scalar phase functions of Stages VII--VIII are diagonal entries of three
positive response Gramians.  For a source \(f\in L^2_0(0,1)\), put

\[
 F_f(s)=\int_0^1e^{-sV(x)}f(x)\,dx.
\]

The resolved, lattice, and atomic Gramians integrate
\(F_f\overline{F_g}\) against their target weights.  If \(V\) is strictly
monotone, all three have trivial null space; for a general modulation their
common null space is the conditional-expectation null generated by \(V\).
Every finite-dimensional quotient is therefore uniformly visible on the
declared compactified atlas, with the uncalibrated lattice sector restricted
to \(\tau\ge\tau_0>0\).  The late resolved and lattice Gramians meet the same
atomic Gram after multiplication by \(\sqrt q\) and \(\sqrt\tau\),
respectively.  At the early resolved endpoint one must instead use a
moment-adapted basis and divide its \(j\)-th response column by \(q^{r_j}\),
where \(r_j\) is its first surviving multiplication moment.  This calibrated
early Gram has a positive Hankel-type limit; a calibrated
\(\tau\downarrow0\) physical lattice theorem is not claimed.

There is also a sharp negative conclusion.  Every chart response is a compact
Laplace-type operator, so an unweighted smallest singular value cannot stay
bounded below as source dimension grows.  In fact the standard chart kernels
are analytic Hankel kernels \(\Phi(V(x)+V(y))\), which gives an exponential
upper ceiling on the smallest \(K\)-port Gram eigenvalue.  Thus the correct
growing-band result is a relative perturbation theorem: if \(\gamma_K\) is
the smallest continuum quotient-Gram eigenvalue and \(\delta_K\) is the
finite-model Gram error, then rank, singular values, and conditioning are
stable in the certified regime

\[
 \boxed{\delta_K=o(\gamma_K).}
\]

Away from the degenerate early endpoint, spatial resolution imposes the
additional source-side restrictions

\[
 K\varepsilon\to0\quad\text{(resolved)},\qquad
 Kh\to0\quad\text{(finite-cell lattice)},\qquad
 K\sqrt t\to0\quad\text{(atomic)}.
\]

At the early endpoint these conditions are not enough.  If the deepest
moment pivot is \(r_d\), mixed source-diffusion--multiplication words require

\[
 \frac{t\|A\|_E}{q^{r_d-1}}\to0;
\]

for the first \(K\) cosines this is
\(tK^2/q^{K-1}\to0\).  Every cancelled discrete moment must also be small
on its own calibrated scale.  Generic midpoint quadrature does not guarantee
this for growing cancellation order; moment-fitted source injection does.

## 2. Source space, null quotient, and finite response Gram

Let

\[
 H_0=\left\{f\in L^2(0,1):\int_0^1f(x)\,dx=0\right\}.
\]

The zero-mean condition is the probability-tangent constraint.  Assume
\(V\in C^2[0,1]\) and \(V\ge v_->0\).  The canonical model is

\[
 V(x)=1+gx,\qquad g>0.
\]

For \(f\in H_0\), define its multiplication response

\[
 \boxed{F_f(s)=\langle1,e^{-sV}f\rangle
 =\int_0^1e^{-sV(x)}f(x)\,dx.}
\]

On the cell-centred grid \(x_i=(i+1/2)h\), let \(I_hf=f_h\) be a declared
source injection, let

\[
 K_{\ell,h}=A_h+\mu_{\ell,h}V_h,\qquad
 a_{\ell,h}(t;f)=
 \langle1,e^{-tK_{\ell,h}}f_h\rangle_h,
\]

and let \(\beta_{\ell,h}\) be the target-preparation coefficient.  The full
bilinear response Gram is

\[
 \boxed{
 G_{h,t}(f,g)=
 \sum_{\ell=1}^{n-1}|\beta_{\ell,h}|^2
 a_{\ell,h}(t;f)\overline{a_{\ell,h}(t;g)}.}
\]

Its diagonal is the squared response norm from Stage VIII.  This bilinear
definition is essential: individual column norms do not determine the
off-diagonal correlations of a Gramian.

### 2.1 The common invisible space

Let \(\mu_V=V_\#(dx)\) and let

\[
 (C_Vf)(v)=\mathbb E[f(X)\mid V(X)=v]
\]

be conditional expectation onto the sigma-algebra generated by \(V\).  The
common continuum null space is

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal Z_V=H_0\cap\ker C_V.}
\]

Indeed, after disintegration,

\[
 F_f(s)=\int e^{-sv}(C_Vf)(v)\,d\mu_V(v).
\]

Uniqueness of the Laplace transform on the compact interval \(V([0,1])\)
shows that \(F_f(s)=0\) for all \(s>0\) if and only if \(C_Vf=0\).  If \(V\)
is strictly monotone, \(C_V\) is injective and \(\mathcal Z_V=\{0\}\).  If
\(V\) is constant, \(\mathcal Z_V=H_0\), recovering the null control from
Stages VII--VIII.

Every smallest-nonzero-singular-value statement below is made on

\[
 E^\sharp=E/(E\cap\mathcal Z_V),
\]

or, equivalently, on the \(L^2\)-orthogonal complement of
\(E\cap\mathcal Z_V\) inside a finite source space \(E\).

## 3. The three continuum Gramians

Let \(\eta\) be the even smooth probability kernel used for resolved target
preparation, and let

\[
 B_Q(\theta)=\sum_mQ_me^{im\theta},\qquad
 \omega(\theta)=4\sin^2(\theta/2).
\]

The continuum chart forms are

\[
 \boxed{
 \Gamma_q^{\mathrm R}(f,g)=
 \int_0^\infty
 |\widehat\eta(\pi u)|^2
 F_f(\pi^2qu^2)\overline{F_g(\pi^2qu^2)}\,du,}
 \tag{R}
\]

\[
 \boxed{
 \Gamma_{\tau,Q}^{\mathrm L}(f,g)=
 \frac1\pi\int_0^\pi
 |B_Q(\theta)|^2
 F_f(\tau\omega(\theta))
 \overline{F_g(\tau\omega(\theta))}\,d\theta,}
 \tag{L}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 \Gamma_\kappa^{\mathrm A}(f,g)=
 \int_0^\infty[1+\cos(2\pi\kappa r)]
 F_f(\pi^2r^2)\overline{F_g(\pi^2r^2)}\,dr.}
 \tag{A}
\]

Here \(\kappa\in[0,\infty)\) is the reflecting-boundary coordinate and
\(\kappa=\infty\) means that the cosine term is absent.  Thus
\(\Gamma_\infty^{\mathrm A}\) is the interior atomic Gram.  For one source
direction \(f=\phi_k\), the diagonal entries are exactly
\(\mathcal P_k(q)^2\), \(\Psi_{Q,k}(\tau)^2\), and
\(C_{F,k,\kappa}^2\).

### Lemma 3.1 — common null space and finite-quotient positivity

Suppose the resolved weight is nonzero on an interval, \(Q\) is a probability
profile, and \(q,\tau>0\).  Then each of (R), (L), and (A) has null space
\(\mathcal Z_V\).  Consequently its restriction to every finite quotient
\(E^\sharp\) is positive definite.

#### Proof

Each form is the squared norm of \(F_f\) in a nonnegative weight.  The
resolved weight is positive on a set with an accumulation point because
\(\widehat\eta(0)=1\).  In the lattice chart, \(B_Q(0)=1\), so its weight is
positive on an interval about zero.  In the atomic chart,
\(1+\cos(2\pi\kappa r)\) is positive almost everywhere; for
\(\kappa=\infty\) it is one.  Vanishing of any form therefore makes the
analytic function \(F_f\) vanish on a set with an accumulation point.  It
vanishes identically, which is equivalent to \(f\in\mathcal Z_V\).  A
positive quadratic form on a finite-dimensional space has a positive
smallest eigenvalue.  \(\square\)

### Lemma 3.2 — both late charts meet the same atomic Gram

For fixed \(f,g\in H_0\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \sqrt q\,\Gamma_q^{\mathrm R}(f,g)
 \longrightarrow\Gamma_\infty^{\mathrm A}(f,g),
 \qquad q\to\infty,}
 \tag{3.1}
\]

and, for every fixed probability profile \(Q\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \sqrt\tau\,\Gamma_{\tau,Q}^{\mathrm L}(f,g)
 \longrightarrow\Gamma_\infty^{\mathrm A}(f,g),
 \qquad \tau\to\infty.}
 \tag{3.2}
\]

The convergence is in operator norm after restriction to a fixed finite
source space.  It is uniform for \(Q\) in a compact family with uniformly
bounded first moment.

#### Proof

In (R), set \(r=\sqrt q\,u\):

\[
 \sqrt q\,\Gamma_q^{\mathrm R}(f,g)
 =\int_0^\infty
 \left|\widehat\eta\left(\frac{\pi r}{\sqrt q}\right)\right|^2
 F_f(\pi^2r^2)\overline{F_g(\pi^2r^2)}\,dr.
\]

The multiplier tends to one.  In (L), set \(z=\sqrt\tau\,\theta\), use
\(\tau\omega(z/\sqrt\tau)\to z^2\) and
\(B_Q(z/\sqrt\tau)\to1\), and finally put \(z=\pi r\).  The bound

\[
 |F_f(s)|\le\|f\|_2e^{-v_-s}
\]

provides an integrable Gaussian majorant.  Dominated convergence proves the
scalar claims.  On a fixed finite source space, entrywise and operator-norm
convergence are equivalent.  Uniformity in \(Q\) follows from

\[
 |B_Q(\theta)-1|\le|\theta|\sum_m|m|Q_m.
\]

\(\square\)

### Lemma 3.3 — compactness

The response operator whose Gram is any one of (R), (L), or (A) is compact
from \(H_0\) to its weighted output space.

#### Proof

In each chart the operator has kernel \(e^{-sV(x)}\) after the displayed
change of variables.  For (R) and (L), its squared kernel is integrable
because the parameter domain or target multiplier is integrable.  For (A),

\[
 \int_0^\infty\int_0^1e^{-2\pi^2r^2V(x)}\,dx\,dr<\infty.
\]

Thus every chart operator is Hilbert--Schmidt.  \(\square\)

### 3.4 Hankel-kernel representation

Fubini gives

\[
 \Gamma(f,g)=\int_0^1\int_0^1
 f(x)\overline{g(y)}\,\Phi(V(x)+V(y))\,dx\,dy.
 \tag{3.3}
\]

Thus every chart Gram is a Hankel kernel in the modulation coordinate.  For
the standard Gaussian
\(\eta(z)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-z^2/2}\), for a one-cell lattice atom
\(Q=\delta_0\), and for the atomic charts, the kernels are exactly

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \Phi_q^{\mathrm R}(A)
 &=\frac{1}{2\sqrt\pi\sqrt{1+qA}},\\
 \Phi_\tau^{\mathrm L}(A)
 &=e^{-2\tau A}I_0(2\tau A),\\
 \Phi_\infty^{\mathrm A}(A)
 &=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi A}},\\
 \Phi_\kappa^{\mathrm A}(A)
 &=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi A}}
   \left(1+e^{-\kappa^2/A}\right).
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{3.4}
\]

The first identity uses
\(|\widehat\eta(\xi)|^2=e^{-\xi^2}\); the second uses the integral
representation of \(I_0\); and the last uses the cosine transform of a
Gaussian.  These formulas are normalization checks: at \(\kappa=0\) the
atomic squared kernel doubles, while \(\kappa\to\infty\) recovers the
interior kernel.

## 4. The early moment calibration

The raw resolved Gram loses rank as \(q\downarrow0\).  A source family
requires a filtration rather than the single odd/even order used in Stage
VIII.

For \(j\ge1\), define

\[
 m_j(f)=\int_0^1V(x)^jf(x)\,dx.
\]

Let \(E^\sharp\) have dimension \(d\), and set

\[
 N_r=\{f\in E^\sharp:m_1(f)=\cdots=m_r(f)=0\},
 \qquad N_0=E^\sharp.
\]

Because polynomials in \(V\) are dense among functions measurable with
respect to \(V\), the chain \(N_r\) eventually reaches zero.  A scalar moment
can lower dimension by at most one.  List the pivot orders

\[
 1\le r_1<r_2<\cdots<r_d
\]

at which dimension drops, and choose a moment-adapted basis
\(e_1,\ldots,e_d\), orthonormal in the quotient \(L^2\) metric, satisfying

\[
 m_j(e_i)=0\quad(j<r_i),\qquad m_{r_i}(e_i)\ne0.
\]

Write

\[
 c_i=\frac{(-1)^{r_i}}{r_i!}m_{r_i}(e_i),\qquad
 D_E(q)=\operatorname{diag}(q^{r_1},\ldots,q^{r_d}).
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — positive calibrated early endpoint

In moment-adapted coordinates,

\[
 \boxed{
 D_E(q)^{-1}\Gamma_q^{\mathrm R}|_{E^\sharp}D_E(q)^{-1}
 \longrightarrow H_E^\eta,\qquad q\downarrow0,}
 \tag{4.1}
\]

where

\[
 \boxed{
 (H_E^\eta)_{ij}=
 c_i\overline{c_j}\,\pi^{2(r_i+r_j)}
 \int_0^\infty
 |\widehat\eta(\pi u)|^2u^{2(r_i+r_j)}\,du.}
 \tag{4.2}
\]

The matrix \(H_E^\eta\) is positive definite.

#### Proof

Taylor expansion at zero gives

\[
 F_{e_i}(s)=c_is^{r_i}+O_E(s^{r_i+1}).
\]

Insert \(s=\pi^2qu^2\) in (R), divide the \(i,j\) entry by
\(q^{r_i+r_j}\), and use the Schwartz decay of \(\widehat\eta\) to dominate
the Taylor remainder.  This proves (4.1).  Its quadratic form is

\[
 \int_0^\infty|\widehat\eta(\pi u)|^2
 \left|\sum_i z_ic_i\pi^{2r_i}u^{2r_i}\right|^2du.
\]

The powers \(u^{2r_i}\) are distinct.  A nonzero polynomial cannot vanish on
an interval where \(\widehat\eta\) is nonzero, so the form is positive
definite.  \(\square\)

Equation (4.1) is a generalized-Gram statement.  It equips
\(f=\sum_i z_ie_i\) with the \(q\)-dependent source metric

\[
 \|f\|_{E,q}^2=\|D_E(q)z\|_2^2.
\]

It does **not** say that the raw \(L^2\)-normalized response stays nonzero as
\(q\to0\).  Raw columns still vanish like \(q^{r_i}\).

### Theorem 4.2 — the ramp/cosine pivot orders are exactly \(1,\ldots,K\)

Let \(V(x)=1+gx\) and

\[
 E_K=\operatorname{span}\{\sqrt2\cos(\pi x),\ldots,
 \sqrt2\cos(K\pi x)\}.
\]

Then the moment map

\[
 f\longmapsto(m_1(f),\ldots,m_K(f))
\]

is an isomorphism \(E_K\to\mathbb R^K\).  Hence the pivot orders are exactly

\[
 \boxed{r_j=j,\qquad 1\le j\le K.}
 \tag{4.3}
\]

If \(\mathcal T_qf(u)=\widehat\eta(\pi u)F_f(\pi^2qu^2)\), its \(K\)
singular values in decreasing order obey, for fixed \(K\),

\[
 \boxed{\sigma_j(\mathcal T_q|_{E_K})\asymp_K q^j,
 \qquad j=1,\ldots,K,\quad q\downarrow0.}
 \tag{4.4}
\]

#### Proof

Since \(V\) is affine, the span of \(1,V,\ldots,V^K\) is the space of
polynomials in \(x\) of degree at most \(K\).  Suppose a nonzero
\(f\in E_K\) were orthogonal to all of them.  Writing
\(f(x)=P(\cos\pi x)\) shows that \(f\) has at most \(K\) sign changes in
\((0,1)\).  Let \(x_1,\ldots,x_m\), \(m\le K\), be its sign-change zeros.
Then a choice of sign makes

\[
 p(x)=\pm\prod_{\nu=1}^m(x-x_\nu)
\]

have the same sign as \(f\) between consecutive sign changes.  Therefore
\(\int_0^1p(x)f(x)\,dx>0\), contradicting orthogonality to every polynomial
of degree at most \(K\).  The moment map is injective and hence bijective.
This proves (4.3).

In the adapted basis,
\(\mathcal T_q=Y(q)D_E(q)\), where \(Y(q)\to Y(0)\) and the \(K\) columns
of \(Y(0)\) are independent by Theorem 4.1.  Exterior powers, or equivalently
successive Schur complements of
\(D_E(q)H_E^\eta D_E(q)\), give (4.4).  \(\square\)

### 4.3 Discrete moment defects

Define

\[
 m_{j,h}(f_h)=\langle1,V_h^jf_h\rangle_h
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 \Delta_{E,h}(q)=
 \max_i\left[
 \sum_{0\le j<r_i}q^{j-r_i}|m_{j,h}(e_{i,h})|
 +|m_{r_i,h}(e_{i,h})-m_{r_i}(e_i)|
 \right].}
 \tag{4.5}
\]

Here \(m_{0,h}=0\) should be enforced by exact mass correction.  The early
finite Gram can converge to (4.2) only if \(\Delta_{E,h}(q)\to0\).
A spurious order-\(j\) moment becomes \(q^{j-r_i}m_{j,h}\) after
calibration.

For midpoint injection of a fixed smooth family,

\[
 |m_{j,h}(e_{i,h})-m_j(e_i)|\le C_{E,j}h^2.
\]

If no cancellation beyond mass is exact, the safe condition is

\[
 \boxed{C_Eh^2q^{1-r_d}\to0.}
 \tag{4.6}
\]

The canonical even cosine ports from Stage VIII are exceptional only at their
first cancellation: reflection makes \(m_{1,h}=0\) exactly.  It does not fit
every higher moment in a growing adapted basis.

A **moment-fitted injection** chooses \(e_{i,h}\) so that

\[
 m_{j,h}(e_{i,h})=0\quad(j<r_i),\qquad
 m_{r_i,h}(e_{i,h})\longrightarrow m_{r_i}(e_i).
\]

For fixed \(E\), it exists on sufficiently fine grids because the discrete
moment matrix converges to the nonsingular triangular continuum matrix.
Uniform construction as \(d\to\infty\) additionally requires control of the
moment-matrix condition number; no such bound is claimed here.

### Corollary 4.3 — a logarithmic resolved-band barrier

At microscopic time \(t=\tau h^2\), the Stage VIII critical width for a port
of moment order \(r\) is

\[
 \alpha_c(r)=\frac{4r}{4r+1},\qquad \varepsilon=h^{\alpha_c(r)}.
\]

The worst direction in \(E_K\) has \(r=K\), so

\[
 \frac{\varepsilon}{h}=h^{-1/(4K+1)}
 =n^{1/(4K+1)}.
\]

Therefore the critical family remains genuinely resolved only if

\[
 \boxed{K=o(\log n).}
 \tag{4.7}
\]

If \(K\sim c\log n\), the resolution ratio tends to the finite constant
\(e^{1/(4c)}\), so the continuum early profile does not separate from the
finite-cell lattice phase.  This is a geometric obstruction independent of
the still-smaller observability floor \(\gamma_K\).  Conversely,
\(K=o(\log n)\) is only a necessary geometric condition.  It does not control
the moment-matrix inverse, mixed \(A\)-\(V\) words, or \(\gamma_K\), and is
not asserted sufficient for a \(K(n)\) finite-grid theorem.

## 5. Uniform coercivity on the compactified finite atlas

Fix an early cutoff \(q_0>0\); a late resolved family
\(q\in[q_0,\infty]\); a lattice family
\(\tau\in[\tau_0,\infty]\), \(\tau_0>0\), with \(Q\) in a compact family of
probability profiles, compact in \(\ell^1\) and with uniformly bounded first
moment; and
\(\kappa\in[0,\infty]\).  Compactify \(q,\tau,\kappa\) at infinity.

Use the normalized Gramians

\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 \text{chart}&\text{normalized continuum Gram}\\ \hline
 \text{early resolved}&D_E(q)^{-1}\Gamma_q^{\mathrm R}D_E(q)^{-1}\\[2mm]
 \text{resolved-to-atomic}&(1+q)^{1/2}\Gamma_q^{\mathrm R}\\[1mm]
 \text{lattice-to-atomic}&(1+\tau)^{1/2}\Gamma_{\tau,Q}^{\mathrm L}\\[1mm]
 \text{atomic/boundary}&\Gamma_\kappa^{\mathrm A}.
 \end{array}
\]

At \(q=0\), use \(H_E^\eta\).  At \(q=\infty\) and \(\tau=\infty\), use
the scalar multiples of \(\Gamma_\infty^{\mathrm A}\) dictated by the
normalization above.

### Theorem 5.1 — finite-source atlas theorem

Let \(E^\sharp\) be finite dimensional.  The normalized Gram family is
continuous on each compactified chart and positive definite at every point.
Hence

\[
 \boxed{
 \gamma_E=
 \inf_{c\in\mathfrak A}
 \lambda_{\min}\bigl(\widehat\Gamma_c|_{E^\sharp}\bigr)>0.}
 \tag{5.1}
\]

Equivalently, the smallest normalized response singular value is uniformly
at least \(\sqrt{\gamma_E}\) on the declared atlas.

#### Proof

Lemmas 3.1--3.2 and Theorem 4.1 give positivity and continuity at finite
points and the early and late endpoints.  Boundary continuity at finite
\(\kappa\) follows by dominated convergence.  As \(\kappa\to\infty\), the
oscillatory term tends to zero by the Riemann--Lebesgue lemma.  Restriction to
a fixed finite space turns form continuity into matrix operator-norm
continuity.  The smallest eigenvalue is a positive continuous function on a
compact parameter set, so its infimum is positive.  \(\square\)

The theorem is uniform in chart parameters, not in source dimension.  The
early statement is uniform in its calibrated jet metric, not in raw \(L^2\).
The lattice family excludes \(\tau\downarrow0\); that endpoint has its own
moment-jet degeneration.

## 6. The growing-band perturbation theorem

Let \(E_K\subset H_0\) be nested finite source spaces, let
\(d_K=\dim E_K^\sharp\), and let \(\widehat\Gamma_{K,c}\) denote the
calibrated continuum Gram in chart \(c\).  Construct the finite Gram with the
same normalization and source calibration, and call it
\(\widehat G_{K,h,c}\).  Define

\[
 \boxed{
 \delta_K(h,\varepsilon,t)=
 \sup_{c\in\mathfrak A(h,\varepsilon,t)}
 \|\widehat G_{K,h,c}-\widehat\Gamma_{K,c}\|_{\mathrm op}.}
 \tag{6.1}
\]

The supremum is over the chart parameters active along the declared path.  It
is a quadratic-form error:

\[
 \|G-H\|_{\mathrm op}
 =\sup_{\|z\|_2=1}|z^*(G-H)z|.
\]

Thus a scalar Stage VIII estimate must hold uniformly for **every** source in
the calibrated unit sphere, not merely for the chosen basis vectors, before
it can be assembled into (6.1).

### Theorem 6.1 — exact spectral stability criterion

Let \(K=K(h,\varepsilon,t)\) and let \(\gamma_K\) be the finite-atlas floor
from (5.1).  If

\[
 \boxed{\delta_K<\gamma_K,}
 \tag{6.2}
\]

then every calibrated finite Gram is positive definite on the continuum
quotient and

\[
 \lambda_{\min}(\widehat G_{K,h,c})
 \ge\gamma_K-\delta_K.
\]

For every index \(j\),

\[
 |\lambda_j(\widehat G_{K,h,c})
   -\lambda_j(\widehat\Gamma_{K,c})|\le\delta_K,
\]

and the corresponding response singular values obey

\[
 \boxed{
 |\sigma_j(\widehat R_{K,h,c})
   -\sigma_j(\widehat R_{K,c})|
 \le\sqrt{\delta_K}.}
 \tag{6.3}
\]

More sharply, in the continuum operational metric,

\[
 \boxed{
 \left\|
 \widehat\Gamma_{K,c}^{-1/2}
 \widehat G_{K,h,c}
 \widehat\Gamma_{K,c}^{-1/2}-I
 \right\|_{\mathrm op}
 \le\frac{\delta_K}{\gamma_K}.}
 \tag{6.4}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{\delta_K/\gamma_K\to0}
 \tag{6.5}
\]

is sufficient for uniform relative singular-value and condition-number
stability across the growing-band atlas.

#### Proof

Weyl's eigenvalue inequality gives (6.2) and the eigenvalue estimate.  For
positive semidefinite matrices,
\(|\sqrt a-\sqrt b|\le\sqrt{|a-b|}\), which gives (6.3).  Finally,

\[
 \|\Gamma^{-1/2}(G-\Gamma)\Gamma^{-1/2}\|
 \le\|\Gamma^{-1}\|\,\|G-\Gamma\|
 \le\delta_K/\gamma_K,
\]

proving (6.4)--(6.5).  \(\square\)

### Corollary 6.2 — a conditional genuinely growing band exists

Along every admissible balanced, finite-cell, or atomic Stage VIII parameter
path there is a sequence \(K(h,\varepsilon,t)\to\infty\), possibly very slow,
for which (6.5) holds.  On an early path the same conclusion holds only if,
for arbitrarily large fixed \(K\), the moment defects and the mixed-word
ratio in (7.6) tend to zero.  Moment-fitted injection supplies the first
condition, not the second.

#### Proof

For every fixed \(K\) admitted by the stated path conditions, the Stage VIII
weak-Duhamel, conservative-target, and lattice arguments apply uniformly to
the compact unit sphere of \(E_K\).
Polarization gives \(\delta_K\to0\), while Theorem 5.1 gives
\(\gamma_K>0\).  Choose successive parameter thresholds at which
\(\delta_K<\gamma_K/K\), and increase \(K\) only after crossing the next
threshold.  This diagonal construction tends to infinity and satisfies
(6.5).  \(\square\)

This corollary is existential, not a useful bandwidth schedule.  An explicit
schedule requires a quantitative lower bound for \(\gamma_K\) and explicit
source-norm tracking in \(\delta_K\).

## 7. Sobolev calibration and safe quantitative budgets

For the Neumann cosine band \(E_K\), define the Sobolev synthesis map

\[
 J_{K,s}z=
 \sum_{k=1}^K
 \frac{z_k}{(1+(k\pi)^2)^{s/2}}\phi_k.
\]

The weighted Gram is \(J_{K,s}^*\Gamma J_{K,s}\).  It represents sources
measured in the \(H^s\) metric.  For \(s>5/2\), the \(H^s\) unit ball is
uniformly bounded in \(C^2\).

### Lemma 7.1 — Sobolev-uniform form lift

On every compact balanced or late chart family, the Stage VIII finite-minus-
continuum Gram form \(B_h=G_h-\Gamma\) satisfies

\[
 |B_h(f,f)|\le C_s\mathcal E_{\rm chart}\|f\|_{H^s}^2,
 \qquad s>5/2,
 \tag{7.1a}
\]

with the chart error budgets displayed below.

#### Proof

Repeat the Stage VIII weak-Duhamel, midpoint-quadrature, dispersion, and
target-multiplier estimates before specializing the source to one cosine.
Every source-dependent factor there is bounded by
\(\|f\|_{C^2}\), and every resulting Gram term is quadratic in \(f\).
The one-dimensional embedding
\(H^s(0,1)\hookrightarrow C^2[0,1]\) for \(s>5/2\) makes the constant
uniform on the \(H^s\) unit ball.  Finally, \(B_h\) is a self-adjoint form, so

\[
 \|B_h\|_{\mathrm op}
 =\sup_{\|f\|_{H^s}=1}|B_h(f,f)|.
\]

This converts the uniform scalar-form estimate into the claimed operator
estimate.  \(\square\)

Consequently the following are safe absolute operator-error budgets:

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \delta_{K,s}^{\mathrm R}
 &\le C_s\left[\varepsilon^2+
             \left(\frac h\varepsilon\right)^2\right],
 &&q\in[q_-,q_+],\\
 \delta_{K,s}^{\mathrm L}
 &\le C_s\left[h+\|w_h-Q\|_1\right],
 &&\tau\in[\tau_-,\tau_+],\\
 \delta_{K,s}^{\mathrm A}
 &\le C_s\left[t+\frac{h^2+\varepsilon^2}{t}\right],
 &&\text{smooth interior preparation}.
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{7.1}
\]

For an arbitrary target probability vector in the simultaneous atomic chart,
the last line is replaced by the safe Stage VIII budget

\[
 \boxed{
 \delta_{K,s}^{\mathrm A}\le C_s\left[
 \sqrt t+h+\frac1\tau+\frac{\sigma_h^2}{t}
 +\frac{\sqrt t}{d_h}\right].}
 \tag{7.2}
\]

The constants in (7.1)--(7.2) are independent of \(K\) in the Sobolev
metric.  These are absolute convergence statements; compactness still
prevents a uniform infinite-dimensional coercivity bound.

For the raw \(L^2\) metric on \(E_K\),

\[
 \|f\|_{H^s}^2\le(1+(K\pi)^2)^s\|f\|_2^2.
\]

Taking the integer-safe choice \(s=3\) turns (7.1) into

\[
 \boxed{\delta_{K,0}\le C(1+K^2)^3\,\mathcal E_{\rm chart}.}
 \tag{7.3}
\]

This \(K^6\) bookkeeping bound is deliberately conservative and is not
claimed sharp.  If a certified continuum computation or a separate analytic
argument supplies \(\gamma_K\ge\underline\gamma_K>0\), then

\[
 \boxed{(1+K^2)^3\mathcal E_{\rm chart}
 =o(\underline\gamma_K)}
 \tag{7.4}
\]

is an explicit sufficient growing-band rule away from the early endpoint.

In the early chart, (7.1) must be augmented by moment calibration.  For fixed
\(E_K\), the proof gives the finite-dimensional bound

\[
 \boxed{
 \delta_K^{\mathrm{early}}
 \le C_K\left[q+\left(\frac h\varepsilon\right)^2+\varepsilon^2
 +\frac{tK^2}{q^{K-1}}+\Delta_{E_K,h}(q)\right].}
 \tag{7.5}
\]

Thus the right side of (7.5) must be \(o(\gamma_K)\).  No \(K\)-uniform bound
on \(C_K\), the deepest pivot order, or the moment-matrix inverse is asserted.
For a general finite source space, replace \(K\) in the mixed-word term by
the deepest pivot \(r_d\), and \(K^2\) by
\(\|A\|_{E_K\to E_K}\):

\[
 \boxed{\frac{t\|A\|_{E_K}}{q^{r_d-1}}\to0.}
 \tag{7.6}
\]

The origin of (7.6) is the Stage VIII weak-Duhamel remainder.  Its leading
mixed word has absolute size \(O(t\|A\|_{E_K}q)\); the deepest calibrated
column has multiplication size \(q^{r_d}\).  Division therefore produces
\(t\|A\|_{E_K}/q^{r_d-1}\).  Moment fitting removes pure multiplication
leakage but cannot remove these noncommutative \(A\)-\(V\) words.

### 7.1 Threshold quotients

Sobolev calibration is most useful with a declared information threshold.
For \(\alpha>0\), retain continuum Gram eigenvectors with eigenvalue at least
\(\alpha\).  If \(\delta_{K,s}<\alpha/2\), every retained direction has
finite response eigenvalue at least \(\alpha/2\).  If the continuum Gram has
no eigenvalue in
\([\alpha-\delta_{K,s},\alpha+\delta_{K,s}]\), the number of directions above
threshold is also stable.  This is the correct infinite-family statement:
fixed operational resolution can stabilize a finite observable quotient even
though it cannot stabilize every Sobolev direction.

## 8. Bandwidth versus the three physical scales

The profile \(F_f\) arises by neglecting source diffusion relative to the
active target frequency.  A growing band makes that approximation
conditional.  For the highest cosine source \(K\), the source Laplacian has
size \(K^2\).  The active target modes and the small coefficient of the source
Laplacian are

\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
 \text{chart}&\text{active target mode}&A/\mu_\ell
 &\text{required low-source condition}\\ \hline
 \text{balanced resolved}&\ell\asymp\varepsilon^{-1}&O(\varepsilon^2)
 &K\varepsilon\to0\\
 \text{early resolved}&\ell\asymp\varepsilon^{-1}&O(\varepsilon^2)
 &tK^2/q^{K-1}\to0\\
 \text{finite-cell lattice}&\ell\asymp h^{-1}&O(h^2)
 &Kh\to0\\
 \text{atomic}&\ell\asymp t^{-1/2}&O(t)
 &K\sqrt t\to0.
 \end{array}
\]

These are safe uniform low-source conditions and are generically necessary
for a theorem that includes the highest band mode without a special
cancellation.  They are the source-side counterparts of
\(h/\varepsilon\to0\) and \(h/\sqrt t\to0\) on the target side.  They follow
by writing the reduced semigroup at \(s=t\mu_\ell\) as

\[
 e^{-s(V+A/\mu_\ell)}.
\]

On \(E_K\), \(A/\mu_\ell\) has size \(K^2/\mu_\ell\).  In balanced and late
charts this gives the displayed low-source conditions.  In the early chart,
however, absolute smallness is insufficient because the \(j\)-th phase
column itself vanishes like \(q^j\).  The leading mixed word has to be small
relative to the deepest \(q^K\) column, producing (7.6).  If these conditions
fail, the Stage VIII multiplication profile \(F_f\) is not the correct
uniform growing-band profile.  A different two-frequency or causal-word phase
may still exist, but it lies outside the theorem proved here.

The full admissibility ledger is

\[
 \begin{array}{c|l}
 \text{chart}&\text{necessary theorem inputs}\\ \hline
 \text{balanced resolved}&
 h/\varepsilon\to0,\ K\varepsilon\to0,\
 \delta_K/\gamma_K\to0\\
 \text{early resolved}&
 h/\varepsilon\to0,\ Kh\to0,\
 tK^2/q^{K-1}\to0,\
 \Delta_{E_K,h}(q)\to0,\
 \delta_K/\gamma_K\to0\\
 \text{finite-cell lattice}&
 Kh\to0,\ w_h\to Q,\
 \delta_K/\gamma_K\to0\\
 \text{atomic interior}&
 h/\sqrt t\to0,\ K\sqrt t\to0,\
 \sigma_h^2/t\to0,\ d_h/\sqrt t\to\infty,\
 \delta_K/\gamma_K\to0\\
 \text{atomic boundary}&
 h/\sqrt t\to0,\ K\sqrt t\to0,\
 \sigma_h^2/t\to0,\ d_h/\sqrt t\to\kappa,\
 \delta_K/\gamma_K\to0.
 \end{array}
\]

### 8.1 The critical singular-value fan

For the ramp/cosine band, Theorem 4.2 and the Stage VIII physical
normalization give, for the continuum resolved response (and for the finite
response whenever the hypotheses of Section 6 hold),

\[
 \boxed{
 \sigma_j(R_{\varepsilon,t}|_{E_K})
 \asymp_K\varepsilon^{-1/2}q^j,\qquad
 j=1,\ldots,K,\quad q=t/\varepsilon^2\downarrow0.}
 \tag{8.1}
\]

Set \(t=\tau h^2\) and \(\varepsilon=h^\alpha\), with fixed \(\tau>0\).
The power of \(h\) in the \(j\)-th singular value is

\[
 e_j(\alpha)=2j(1-\alpha)-\frac\alpha2.
\]

Hence its critical ridge is

\[
 \boxed{\alpha_j=\frac{4j}{4j+1}.}
 \tag{8.2}
\]

The \(j\)-th channel vanishes for \(\alpha<\alpha_j\), has a finite nonzero
limit on \(\alpha=\alpha_j\), and diverges for \(\alpha>\alpha_j\), all in
the declared density normalization.  Between \(\alpha_j\) and
\(\alpha_{j+1}\), exactly the first \(j\) asymptotic channels are finite or
divergent; the rest vanish.  Away from a ridge, the visible-dimension
staircase is

\[
 \boxed{
 d_{\mathrm{crit}}(\alpha)=
 \left\lfloor\frac{\alpha}{4(1-\alpha)}\right\rfloor,}
 \tag{8.3}
\]

truncated by \(K\).  At an integer value of the fraction, the last counted
channel is finite rather than divergent.  This staircase concerns physical
amplitude only; distinguishability in noise is further limited by
\(\gamma_K\).

On the \(K\)-th critical ridge, the mixed-word ratio is

\[
 \frac{tK^2}{q^{K-1}}
 =K^2\tau^{\,2-K}
 h^{(6K+4)/(4K+1)}.
\]

It tends to zero for every fixed \(K\).  This verifies consistency of the
fixed-\(K\) critical fan with the physical finite-model reduction.  It does
not prove a uniform \(K=K(n)\) result: the constants, number of mixed words,
moment calibration, and observability floor all depend on \(K\).

## 9. Why a dimension-independent floor is impossible

### Theorem 9.1 — no unweighted infinite-band coercivity

Let \(E_K\) be nested with dense union in an infinite-dimensional subspace of
\(H_0/\mathcal Z_V\).  For every fixed chart point,

\[
 \boxed{
 \lambda_{\min}(\Gamma|_{E_K^\sharp})\longrightarrow0.}
 \tag{9.1}
\]

The same holds for any fixed Sobolev weighting whose synthesis into \(L^2\)
is bounded.

#### Proof

By Lemma 3.3, the response operator \(T\) is compact.  Choose an orthonormal
sequence \(u_K\) with \(u_K\in E_K^\sharp\) and
\(u_K\rightharpoonup0\).  Compactness gives \(Tu_K\to0\), while

\[
 \lambda_{\min}(T^*T|_{E_K^\sharp})\le\|Tu_K\|^2.
\]

Composing \(T\) with a bounded Sobolev synthesis remains compact.
\(\square\)

### Theorem 9.2 — analytic Hankel kernels give an exponential ceiling

Let \(K\ge2\), equip \(E_K\) with its \(L^2\) source metric, and let
\(I=[2v_-,2v_+]\), where
\(v_+=\|V\|_\infty\), and suppose the chart
kernel \(\Phi\) in (3.3) extends analytically to a complex neighborhood of
\(I\).  Then there are \(C<\infty\) and \(\rho>1\) such that, for **every**
\(K\)-dimensional source space \(E_K\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \lambda_{\min}(\Gamma|_{E_K})
 \le C\rho^{-K}.}
 \tag{9.2}
\]

The constants can be chosen uniformly on compact chart-parameter families
whose kernels share one complex neighborhood and a uniform analytic bound.
The standard Gaussian resolved, fixed-\(\tau\) one-cell lattice, interior
atomic, and compact-\(\kappa\) boundary kernels in (3.4) satisfy the
hypothesis.

#### Proof

Analytic polynomial approximation on \(I\) supplies polynomials \(p_m\) with

\[
 \sup_{A\in I}|\Phi(A)-p_m(A)|\le C\rho^{-m}.
\]

If \(p_m(A)=\sum_{\ell=0}^ma_\ell A^\ell\), then
\(p_m(V(x)+V(y))\) expands into products \(V(x)^aV(y)^b\) with
\(a+b\le m\).  Its integral operator has range in
\(\operatorname{span}\{1,V,\ldots,V^m\}\), hence rank at most \(m+1\).
Choose \(m=K-2\).  The compression of this approximating operator to the
\(K\)-dimensional space \(E_K\) has a nonzero null vector \(f\).  On that
unit vector, the true Gram Rayleigh quotient is bounded by the operator norm
of the kernel error, at most its Hilbert--Schmidt norm, which is
\(C\rho^{-K}\).  \(\square\)

The ceiling (9.2) is stronger than compactness, but it is still only an upper
bound.  It does not supply the lower bound on \(\gamma_K\) required for an
explicit stable schedule.  Uniformity all the way to singular chart
parameters must be checked after the chart normalization; it is not automatic
from pointwise analyticity.

Since the \(L^2\)-source atlas floor is an infimum over chart points,
inclusion of any one analytic fixed chart gives the same exponential ceiling
for \(\gamma_K\).
Therefore (6.5) necessarily forces

\[
 \delta_K\rho^K\longrightarrow0
\]

for at least one \(\rho>1\) supplied by that chart.  An error budget that is
only polynomially small in the mesh can consequently support at most a
logarithmic-order band.  This is a necessary ceiling, not a sufficient
schedule: the unknown \(\gamma_K\) may decay faster.

### Proposition 9.3 — elementary cosine-port ceiling

For \(V(x)=1+gx\) and
\(\phi_k=\sqrt2\cos(k\pi x)\),

\[
 F_k(s)=\sqrt2e^{-s}
 \frac{gs[1-(-1)^ke^{-gs}]}{(gs)^2+(k\pi)^2}.
\]

On compact resolved or lattice parameter families and throughout the
compactified atomic boundary family,

\[
 \Gamma(\phi_k,\phi_k)\le Ck^{-4}.
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{\gamma_K\le CK^{-4},\qquad
 \sigma_{\min}(R|_{E_K})\le CK^{-2}.}
 \tag{9.3}
\]

#### Proof

The explicit formula gives
\(|F_k(s)|\le Ck^{-2}se^{-s}\).  Its square is integrable against every
declared chart measure.  Test the Rayleigh quotient on \(\phi_K\).
\(\square\)

The analytic ceiling (9.2) explains why correlations can make the true floor
much smaller than the elementary diagonal ceiling (9.3).

## 10. Counterexamples and sharp boundaries

### 10.1 Column norms do not determine a Gramian

The pairs of output columns \((u,u)\) and \((u,-u)\) have identical
individual norms but opposite off-diagonal Gram entries.  Diagonal Stage VIII
norm estimates cannot alone control singular values.  Uniform quadratic-form
estimates, cross-response estimates, or polarization on the entire source
unit sphere are required.

### 10.2 Absolute convergence does not stabilize a vanishing eigenvalue

The matrices

\[
 G_K=\operatorname{diag}(1,\gamma_K),\qquad
 \widetilde G_K=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)
\]

satisfy \(\|G_K-\widetilde G_K\|=\gamma_K\to0\), but their ranks differ.
Thus \(\delta_K\to0\) is insufficient; the relevant condition is
\(\delta_K/\gamma_K\to0\).

### 10.3 A mesh-scale source changes the limiting profile

If \(K\asymp h^{-1}\) in the lattice chart, \(h^2A_h\) has an order-one
eigenvalue on the highest source ports.  The reduced operator converges to a
multiplication-plus-source-diffusion operator, not multiplication alone.
This is the growing-band version of the Stage VI counterexample
\(k=n/2,\ t=h^2\).

### 10.4 A cancelled continuum moment can reappear discretely

If \(m_j(e)=0\) but \(m_{j,h}(e_h)\asymp h^2\), an order-\(r>j\)
calibrated column contains the spurious term \(h^2q^{j-r}\).  Choosing
\(q\ll h^{2/(r-j)}\) makes it dominate.  Geometric resolution
\(h/\varepsilon\to0\) does not repair this algebraic defect.

### 10.5 Null spaces belong to the quotient

For non-injective \(V\), functions with \(C_Vf=0\) are exactly invisible in
every chart.  Calling their tiny computed eigenvalues “small but nonzero”
creates a mesh-dependent false rank.  Remove the analytic null space before
making a smallest-nonzero-eigenvalue claim.

### 10.6 No raw uniform theorem at the early endpoint

Even for one visible port,
\(\Gamma_q^{\mathrm R}(f,f)\asymp q^{2r(f)}\) as \(q\downarrow0\).  A raw
\(L^2\)-source coercivity floor across \(q=0\) is impossible.  The
moment-weighted metric is part of the statement, not an optional proof device.

### 10.7 Moment fitting does not cancel mixed causal words

Even if \(m_{j,h}(e_h)=0\) exactly for every \(j<r\), the Dyson expansion of
\(e^{-(tA+sV)}\) contains words such as \(AV^{j}\) and \(V^{j}A\).  They are
not determined by the pure multiplication moments \(m_j\).  Relative to an
order-\(r\) phase column, the weak-Duhamel bound contains
\(t\|A\|_E/q^{r-1}\).  A theorem that imposes only moment fitting and
\(K\varepsilon\to0\) can therefore fail for \(r\ge3\).

### 10.8 A finite output frame imposes a rank ceiling

The chart Gramians above use the complete target-density response.  If an
experiment retains only \(M\) scalar target/time samples, its response matrix
has rank at most \(M\), regardless of source visibility.  A \(K\)-direction
theorem with \(K>M\) is then impossible.  With \(M\ge K\), one must still
assume or prove a lower frame bound for the sampled weighted monomials; the
continuum Gram floor does not supply it automatically.

## 11. What is proved, and what remains open

### Proved in this memo

1. The resolved, finite-cell lattice, and atomic scalar phase laws assemble
   into explicit bilinear response Gramians.
2. All three have the same analytic null quotient; the ramp has no nontrivial
   null direction.
3. The late resolved and late lattice Gramians converge to the same interior
   atomic Gram.
4. A moment-adapted early calibration has an explicit positive Hankel-type
   endpoint Gram.
5. For the affine ramp and first \(K\) cosines, the pivot orders are exactly
   \(1,\ldots,K\), yielding early singular values \(q,\ldots,q^K\) and the
   limiting-phase critical fan \(\alpha_j=4j/(4j+1)\).
6. Every fixed finite quotient has a positive singular-value floor uniformly
   on the compactified, calibrated three-chart atlas.
7. The exact finite-to-continuum stability criterion is
   \(\delta_K/\gamma_K\to0\), with eigenvalue, singular-value, rank, and
   operational-metric consequences.
8. A genuinely growing, though not explicitly scheduled, source band exists
   by a diagonal argument.
9. Sobolev calibration gives dimension-independent **absolute** chart errors,
   and the raw cosine band inherits a safe non-sharp \(K^6\) error factor.
10. Source bandwidth must stay below the active target frequency:
    \(K\varepsilon\to0\), \(Kh\to0\), or \(K\sqrt t\to0\), while the early
    calibrated transfer additionally needs
    \(tK^2/q^{K-1}\to0\).
11. Compactness forbids a dimension-independent unweighted coercivity floor;
    analytic chart kernels give an exponential upper ceiling.

### Not proved here

1. A useful analytic lower bound on \(\gamma_K\) for the first \(K\) cosine
   ports.  This is the main missing ingredient for an explicit maximal
   bandwidth schedule.
2. Sharp \(K\)-dependence of the finite-model error.  The \(K^6\) estimate is
   safe Sobolev bookkeeping, not an optimal exponent.
3. Uniform conditioning of the moment-fitting matrix as \(K\to\infty\).
4. A \(K\)-uniform bound for mixed source-diffusion--multiplication words in
   the early causal flag.
5. The two-frequency limit when \(K\varepsilon\), \(Kh\), or \(K\sqrt t\)
   tends to a nonzero constant.  That regime retains source diffusion.
6. The \(\tau\downarrow0\) lattice endpoint for a growing moment filtration.
7. Regularization choice, composite-hypothesis testing, and minimax recovery
   rates.  The synthesis records only the elementary whitened Gaussian
   simple-versus-simple testing consequence of a certified Gram floor.
8. Growing-band stability after replacing the complete target response by a
   finite sensor/time frame.
9. Any claim about fundamental spacetime.  The result is internal to the
   declared parabolic operational model.

## 12. Recommended next theorem after the computational audit

The immediate analytic target is now precise:

\[
 \boxed{\text{bound }\gamma_K\text{ from below for a calibrated cosine or
 moment-adapted source family}.}
\]

The accompanying laboratory now computes all three continuum Gram matrices,
compares raw and calibrated metrics, tests the finite-grid cutoff, and measures
the spectrum in high precision.  It does not supply outward-rounded lower
bounds.  The next quantitative work should:

1. certify \(\lambda_{\min}>0\) for moderate \(K\) with interval or
   outward-rounded arithmetic;
2. seek useful two-sided asymptotics for \(\gamma_K\);
3. sharpen the \(K\)-dependence of (7.4)--(7.5);
4. deliberately cross \(K\varepsilon\asymp1\), \(Kh\asymp1\), and
   \(K\sqrt t\asymp1\) to identify the missing two-frequency phase; and
5. optimize multi-time and multi-profile sensing for thresholded effective
   rank.

Every audit must report both the absolute spectrum and the spectrum in the
declared source metric.  Whitening can make a finite Gram look perfectly
conditioned, but the norm of the whitening map records the true amplification
cost and must never be omitted.

## 13. Final boundary statement

The growing-band milestone is not a theorem that every new source direction
remains uniformly observable.  Such a theorem is false.  The valid result is
an atlas of finite observable quotients, together with an exact rule for how
quickly the quotient may grow:

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{grow only while discretization error}
 \ll\text{continuum observability floor}.}
\]

The three physical charts determine the discretization error;
multiplication moments determine the early calibration; the continuum Gram
spectrum determines the observability floor.  This separates geometry,
resolution, and conditioning without hiding any of them inside a single
scalar norm.
