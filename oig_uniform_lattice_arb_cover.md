# A proof-producing compact lattice-time cover

## Result

For the exactly centred, odd \(n=1001\), two-port noncommuting Neumann
benchmark, one Arb Taylor cell certifies the complete interval

\[
 \boxed{1\le \tau\le \frac65.}
\]

The certificate retains the actual matrices

\[
 A_{n,\ell}=L_n+\omega_{n,\ell}V_n
\]

and transfers the exact Stage X uniform continuum floors in all three source
comparisons:

| metric | outward spectral-error upper bound | exact-floor decimal | transferred floor |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| \(L^2\) | \(1.86417779915\times10^{-8}\) | \(1.43151965121\times10^{-7}\) | \(>1.24510187129\times10^{-7}\) |
| declared continuum \(H^1\) | \(1.64080518010\times10^{-9}\) | \(3.55231002210\times10^{-9}\) | \(>1.91150484201\times10^{-9}\) |
| natural discrete \(H^1\) | \(1.54750212864\times10^{-9}\) | \(3.55231002210\times10^{-9}\) | \(>2.00480789346\times10^{-9}\) |

The table prints decimal summaries only. The checker compares directed Arb
endpoints against the exact rational floors imported from the Stage X proof
trace. Binary64 decides no inequality.

This is a genuine interval theorem, not a scan of selected \(\tau\) values.
It is also the largest interval executed and verified in this experiment,
not a claim that \(6/5\) is an optimal endpoint.

## 1. Why direct interval evaluation is inadequate

If the finite and continuum Grams are independently evaluated with
\(\tau\) replaced by an interval ball, their physical variation appears in
both boxes. Subtracting the boxes loses their nearly complete cancellation.
For example, on an interval of radius \(10^{-4}\) around \(\tau=1\), even
one continuum raw-Gram entry acquires radius about \(4.5\times10^{-7}\).
That already exceeds the \(K=2\) transfer floors before the finite enclosure
is included.

The useful object to enclose is therefore the error

\[
 \Delta_n(\tau)=G_n(\tau)-G(\tau)
\]

as a common Taylor model.

## 2. Finite Taylor coefficients

The cover centre and radius are

\[
 \tau_c=\frac{11}{10},\qquad r=\frac1{10}.
\]

For each retained even target mode, put

\[
 c(\omega)=2+\frac75\omega,\qquad
 B=A-cI,\qquad
 \rho(\omega)=2+\frac25\omega.
\]

At \(\tau_c\), the checker applies the degree-48 polynomial

\[
 e^{-\tau_c c}
 \sum_{j=0}^{48}\frac{(-\tau_cB)^j}{j!}
\]

to both DCT source ports. Its operator remainder is

\[
 \left\|R_{48}\right\|
 \le
 e^{-\tau_c\omega}
 \frac{(\tau_c\rho)^{49}}{49!}.
 \tag{2.1}
\]

Across all 500 retained modes, the largest directed upper endpoint of (2.1)
is

\[
 3.90881339024\times10^{-36}.
\]

The Taylor coefficients through order 18 are then generated by the exact
recursion

\[
 \frac{1}{q!}\frac{d^q}{d\tau^q}e^{-\tau A}
 =
 \frac{(-A)^q}{q!}e^{-\tau A}.
\]

The base remainder is propagated using
\(\|A\|\le4+(9/5)\omega\le56/5\). All tridiagonal actions, sums, products,
normalizations, and tail additions are performed with Arb balls.

Exact centre parity is used algebraically:

\[
 \beta_\ell=0\quad(\ell\text{ odd}),\qquad
 \beta_\ell^2=\frac2n\quad(\ell\ge2\text{ even}).
\]

The zero mode has zero response because both ports have zero mass.

## 3. Continuum Taylor coefficients

For

\[
 F_k(s)=\sqrt2e^{-s}
 \frac{gs[1-(-1)^ke^{-gs}]}{(gs)^2+(k\pi)^2},
 \qquad g=\frac45,
\]

the checker implements elementary Arb power-series arithmetic for products,
inverses, exponentials, and square roots. It forms the order-18 Taylor
coefficients of

\[
 \sqrt{1+\tau}\,
 F_i(\tau\omega(\xi))F_j(\tau\omega(\xi))
\]

at \(\tau_c\), then rigorously integrates every coefficient over
\(0\le\xi\le1\) with Arb's validated complex integrator.

Subtracting the continuum coefficients from the finite coefficients before
interval evaluation preserves the \(O(n^{-2})\) cancellation found in the
general-\(\tau\) laboratory.

## 4. Uniform Taylor remainder

Let \(M_f=56/5\) and \(M_c=36/5\). Contraction gives, for either response
family,

\[
 |b^{(q)}(\tau)|\le M^q.
\]

Hence an entry \(R_{ij}=b_ib_j\) obeys

\[
 |R_{ij}^{(q)}|\le(2M)^q.
\]

Writing \(c(\tau)=\sqrt{1+\tau}\), Leibniz gives the explicit entry bound

\[
 \left|(cR_{ij})^{(d)}\right|
 \le
 \sum_{j=0}^d
 \binom dj
 \sup_{[1,6/5]}|c^{(j)}|
 (2M)^{d-j}.
 \tag{4.1}
\]

The checker uses (4.1) at \(d=19\) separately for the finite and continuum
families and adds

\[
 \frac{r^{19}}{19!}
 \left(B_{19,f}+B_{19,c}\right)
\]

as a symmetric Arb ball to every Taylor-evaluated entry. The unwhitened
entry remainder is at most

\[
 6.76006250095\times10^{-11}.
\]

After declared \(H^1\) whitening, the entry remainders range from
\(1.67004\times10^{-12}\) to \(6.21924\times10^{-12}\).

These bounds are intentionally coarse but fully explicit. The remaining
width in the final eigenvalue enclosures mainly comes from evaluating the
error polynomial over \([-1/10,1/10]\), not from (2.1).

## 5. Spectral and metric step

For \(L^2\) and declared \(H^1\), both finite and continuum coefficients are
whitened with the same declared metric. For natural discrete \(H^1\), the
finite family uses

\[
 S_n=\operatorname{diag}
 \left(1+n^2\omega_{n,1},1+n^2\omega_{n,2}\right),
\]

while the continuum family uses

\[
 S=\operatorname{diag}(1+\pi^2,1+4\pi^2).
\]

Thus the natural certificate directly compares the generalized finite
spectrum to the continuum generalized spectrum; it does not silently assign
the continuum cost to the finite ports.

For every interval error matrix

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}a&b\\b&d\end{pmatrix},
\]

the checker encloses

\[
 \lambda_\pm=\frac{a+d\pm\sqrt{(a-d)^2+4b^2}}2
\]

with Arb and takes the larger directed absolute endpoint. A strict comparison
with the corresponding exact Stage X floor yields each transferred floor in
the opening table.

## 6. Scope and remaining gap

What is proved:

- the full noncommuting finite model, not a phase surrogate;
- exactly centred atomic target on the odd \(1001\)-cell grid;
- the complete compact interval \(1\le\tau\le6/5\);
- two source ports;
- \(L^2\), declared continuum \(H^1\), and natural discrete \(H^1\);
- a strictly positive finite generalized spectral floor throughout the
  interval.

What is not yet proved:

- the remainder of the requested compact sector \(6/5<\tau\le2\);
- any fixed-\(n\) transfer on the unbounded half-line, which the preceding
  obstruction theorem shows is impossible;
- a proof that the canonical endpoint \(6/5\) is maximal;
- broader bands, off-centre targets, or other generators.

A single Taylor cell covering \([1,2]\) has radius \(1/2\). With the present
global derivative majorant, the Taylor order must be approximately 45 or
higher before its \(H^1\) remainder falls below \(10^{-9}\). The validated
continuum implementation integrates three scalar coefficients separately at
every order; an order-12 centre already takes about 5.3 seconds locally, and
the full finite coefficient construction also grows with order. More
importantly, a high-order interval polynomial over radius \(1/2\) may lose
additional dependency width, so computation alone does not guarantee that
one cell closes.

The practical next step is a piecewise cover, reusing order 12--18 local
Taylor models over adaptively sized rational cells. The current implementation
recomputes all 500 finite target modes at every centre, so covering
\([1,2]\) this way needs a cache or parallel centre evaluation. The proved
\([1,6/5]\) cell establishes that the method works and quantifies the
remaining engineering problem.

## 7. Reproduction

Install the isolated dependency and run:

    python -m pip install -r oig_uniform_lattice_arb_cover_requirements.txt
    python oig_uniform_lattice_arb_cover.py \
      --precision-bits 256 \
      --side-length 1001 \
      --centre 11/10 \
      --radius 1/10 \
      --tau-order 18 \
      --exponential-degree 48 \
      --output /tmp/oig-uniform-arb-cover.json
    python -m unittest -v test_oig_uniform_lattice_arb_cover.py

The canonical 256-bit certificate took about 53 seconds on the local
machine. An independent 192-bit repetition is part of the focused test
module.
