## Abstract

Arithmetic Sensing III optimized positive midpoint quadratures for recovering
the first coefficients of a Dirichlet series, but left six concrete questions:
continuous rather than sampled positivity, sharper treatment of sampling
aliases, harmonic and cutoff continuation, joint selection of the sampling
ratio, fixed-degree number-field envelopes, and measurement channels richer
than the Dedekind zeta function.

This paper carries out all six steps. First, positivity and a density cap are
enforced as semi-infinite constraints by exact extremum separation, after
which Fejer--Riesz factors give whole-interval nonnegativity certificates.
Second, Dirichlet's hyperbola identity and Abel summation yield a divisor-mass
bound on each log-frequency interval. Combining it with periodic midpoint-
kernel envelopes produces an alias-by-alias infinite-tail certificate. For the
published eight-harmonic design this reduces the worst quadratic coefficient
bound at 5,000 samples from `2.32014e-4` to `4.56318e-5`.

Continuation through `H=2,4,6,8` shows monotone improvement on frequencies
held out from the optimization, and cutoff continuation through
`M_d=100,150,200,300` shows coefficient stabilization. A discrete joint
search finds that `m/T=4.42`, just above the analytic million-term alias-safety
threshold, retains a `4.58959e-5` complete bound with 580 fewer samples than
`m/T=5`. The coefficient envelope is extended from the quadratic divisor
function to `d_d`; at the same sensing parameters it certifies integer
recovery for every number field of degrees two, three, and four, while degree
five is not certified. Finally, the Perlis pair generated by `x^8-33` and
`x^8-528` has identical Dedekind zeta data but is separated by the multiset of
dimensions of its `Q_2`-algebra components.

All standard ingredients are identified as such. The contribution here is
their synthesis into an executable sensing certificate and the resulting
computational findings; no claim about zeta zeros, new physics, or general
number-field identification is made.

## 1. Sensing model and the open tasks

Let

\[
F(s)=\sum_{n\ge 1}a(n)n^{-s},\qquad s=\sigma+it,
\quad \sigma>1,
\]

and suppose the target coefficients are `a(1),...,a(N)`. At midpoint times

\[
t_j=(j+\tfrac12)T/m,
\]

we use normalized weights

\[
w_j=\frac1m p(2\pi t_j/T),\qquad
p(\theta)=1+2\sum_{r=1}^{H}c_r\cos(r\theta).
\]

For positive weights, the response kernel

\[
K(\omega)=\sum_jw_j e^{it_j\omega}
\]

is a characteristic function and hence `|K(omega)|<=1`. The target Gram
matrix has entries

\[
G_{n,k}=K(\log(n/k)).
\]

The previous manuscript optimized the coefficients `c_r` subject to finite
conditioning and leakage constraints. Its six next steps are the six sections
below.

## 2. Whole-interval positivity

### 2.1 Exact separation of the semi-infinite constraint

Write `x=cos(theta)`. Then

\[
p(\theta)=P(x)=1+2\sum_{r=1}^{H}c_rT_r(x),
\]

where `T_r` is the Chebyshev polynomial. Therefore every extremum on the
circle is found by evaluating `P` at `x=+-1` and at every real root of `P'` in
`[-1,1]`. This gives an exact finite separation oracle at the level of the
mathematical problem.

The optimizer begins with a small angular grid, solves the linear program,
finds all extrema of the candidate density, adds every violating extremum, and
resolves. It terminates only when

\[
\mu\le p(\theta)\le C-\mu
\quad\hbox{for all }\theta.
\]

The reference solve uses `mu=1e-8` and `C=2.5`. With both endpoints in the
initial constraint set, every continuation instance reported here terminates
after one solve. The earlier Stage III reference vector also passes continuous
positivity: its minimum is `1.17648e-5`; however, its continuous maximum is
`2.500000474`, revealing a small endpoint violation of the nominal cap that a
midpoint-only audit could not see.

### 2.2 Fejer--Riesz certificate

The classical Fejer--Riesz theorem says that a nonnegative trigonometric
polynomial has a spectral factor

\[
p(\theta)=|q(e^{i\theta})|^2.
\]

We produce factors for `p-mu/2` and `C-mu/2-p`. If the largest Fourier-
coefficient reconstruction residual is `rho`, then the uniform residual is at
most `(2H+1)rho`. Consequently

\[
p(\theta)\ge \mu/2-(2H+1)\rho,
\]

and the same lower bound holds for `C-p`.

For the reference design, factoring with `mu/2=5e-9` gives

| certificate | coefficient residual | certified uniform floor |
|---|---:|---:|
| `p-5e-9` | `3.43e-16` | `4.999994e-9` |
| `2.5-5e-9-p` | `8.33e-16` | `4.999986e-9` |

Thus the full continuum, not merely the midpoint weights, is positive and
bounded by 2.5. The numerical residual calculation is independently checked
coefficient by coefficient. It is a strong floating-point certificate, not a
proof-assistant or outward-rounded interval proof.

## 3. Divisor mass on a log-frequency interval

Let

\[
D(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\tau(n).
\]

For `x>=4`, put `r=floor(sqrt(x))`. Dirichlet's hyperbola identity gives

\[
D(x)=2\sum_{a\le r}\lfloor x/a\rfloor-r^2.
\]

Using

\[
\log r+\gamma\le H_r
\le\log r+\gamma+\frac1{2r}
\]

and `sqrt(x)-1<=r<=sqrt(x)`, one obtains the elementary bounds

\[
L(x)\le D(x)\le U(x),
\]

where

\[
\begin{aligned}
L(x)&=x\log x+(2\gamma-1)x-4\sqrt{x}-4,\\
U(x)&=x\log x+(2\gamma-1)x+3\sqrt{x}+1.
\end{aligned}
\]

Negative values of `L` may harmlessly be replaced by zero.

### Theorem A — interval divisor-tail bound

For `4<=X<Y` and `sigma>1`,

\[
\sum_{X<n\le Y}\frac{\tau(n)}{n^\sigma}
\le
Y^{-\sigma}U(Y)-X^{-\sigma}\max(0,L(X))
+\sigma\int_X^Y U(t)t^{-\sigma-1}\,dt.
\]

#### Proof

Abel summation gives the identity

\[
\sum_{X<n\le Y}\tau(n)n^{-\sigma}
=Y^{-\sigma}D(Y)-X^{-\sigma}D(X)
+\sigma\int_X^YD(t)t^{-\sigma-1}\,dt.
\]

Use the upper estimate at `Y` and in the integral, and the lower estimate at
`X`. Every integral is elementary. The implementation evaluates it in the log
domain, so remote alias intervals do not require enumeration. QED.

The inequality was additionally falsification-tested against thousands of
exact randomly selected finite intervals through 200,000. Those tests support
the implementation but are not part of the proof.

## 4. Alias-by-alias infinite tails

The midpoint grid has alias period

\[
A=2\pi m/T.
\]

For the uniform midpoint kernel `U_m`, let `d(omega,A Z)` be the distance to
the nearest alias. On any interval contained within half an alias period,

\[
|U_m(\omega)|
\le \min\left(1,\frac{\pi}{T d(\omega,A\mathbb Z)}\right).
\]

This follows from the exact sine quotient and
`|sin x|>=2|x|/pi` for `|x|<=pi/2`. The cosine window is a finite signed sum of
shifted uniform kernels; the triangle inequality yields an interval supremum,
and positive weights impose the final cap one.

### Theorem B — alias-band remainder

Set `v=log(k/n)` and partition every alias period into finitely many intervals
`I`. For each interval let `B_I` be the kernel supremum above and let
`S_I` be Theorem A applied to the corresponding integer-norm interval. Then

\[
\sum_{k>M}\tau(k)k^{-\sigma}|K(\log(k/n))|
\le \sum_I B_I S_I+R_{\rm remote}.
\]

Here `R_remote` is the elementary complete divisor tail after the last
partitioned alias band. This remains valid at every exact alias, where the
kernel is necessarily one. The computation uses 2,048 bins per alias period,
explicitly treats two full positive aliases, and pays the analytic tail only
after `2.5A`.

At truncation one million, the global unweighted remainder is
`1.5969850e-5`. The alias-band remainders for the reference window are

| target `n` | bandwise remainder | improvement over global `L1` |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | `8.86579e-9` | 1801x |
| 10 | `1.03063e-8` | 1550x |
| 50 | `1.18234e-8` | 1351x |

After the exact million-term kernel sum and Gram inversion, the complete
quadratic comparison is

| window | worst coefficient bound | improvement over Hann |
|---|---:|---:|
| Hann | `2.70048e-2` | 1.0x |
| balanced optimized, cap 2.0 | `3.09709e-4` | 87.2x |
| continuum-certified, cap 2.5 | `4.56318e-5` | 591.8x |

The final value is 5.08 times smaller than the previous coarse-alias result
for the same 5,000 samples. It is also slightly below the former 100,000-sample
tail floor, showing that remote-tail analysis—not raw sampling—was the
bottleneck in that comparison.

## 6. Joint selection of `m/T`

For a million-term finite certificate to lie in the pre-alias region for all
targets, it is sufficient that

\[
\pi\frac mT-H\frac{2\pi}{T}>\log(1,000,001).
\]

At `T=1000` and `H=8`, this requires

\[
\frac mT>4.4131\ldots.
\]

We re-solved the continuum LP at each point of a discrete grid, rather than
merely reusing the same coefficients. Each row below uses `M_d=150` and the
same independent million-term audit.

| `m/T` | `m` | effective samples | complete bound | `lambda_min` |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4.42 | 4420 | 2439.66 | `4.58959e-5` | 0.981816 |
| 4.50 | 4500 | 2483.82 | `4.58643e-5` | 0.981816 |
| 5.00 | 5000 | 2759.79 | `4.57522e-5` | 0.981816 |
| 6.00 | 6000 | 3311.75 | `4.56388e-5` | 0.981816 |

The optimized coefficient vectors agree to roughly `1e-8` across the grid.
Moving from ratio 4.42 to 6.0 spends 1,580 additional samples for only a 0.56%
improvement in the complete deterministic tail bound.

### Alias-threshold economy hypothesis

For fixed `N,sigma,T,H`, density cap, and conditioning floor, suppose `m` is
large compared with `H`. Then the finite pre-alias optimization converges to an
`m`-independent continuum window. Under a fixed certificate cutoff, a
sample-cost-optimal design should therefore choose `m/T` only modestly above
the analytic alias-safety threshold; beyond it, the arithmetic finite-tail
floor dominates and extra samples mainly improve sensor-noise averaging.

The four-point grid and coefficient stability support this hypothesis. They do
not prove monotonicity, asymptotic convergence, or global cost optimality.

## 7. Fixed-degree number-field envelopes

Let `K` have degree `d`, and write

\[
\zeta_K(s)=\sum_{n\ge1}a_K(n)n^{-s}.
\]

At a prime power, the Euler factor shows

\[
0\le a_K(p^r)\le {r+d-1\choose d-1}=d_d(p^r).
\]

Multiplicativity therefore gives

\[
0\le a_K(n)\le d_d(n),
\qquad
\sum_{n\ge1}d_d(n)n^{-s}=\zeta(s)^d.
\]

### Theorem C — elementary fixed-degree remote tail

Let `D_d(x)=sum_(n<=x)d_d(n)`. Induction under Dirichlet convolution gives

\[
D_d(x)\le x(1+\log x)^{d-1}.
\]

Hence, for `X>=1` and `sigma>1`,

\[
\sum_{n>X}d_d(n)n^{-\sigma}
\le
\sigma\int_X^\infty t^{-\sigma}(1+\log t)^{d-1}\,dt.
\]

For fixed integer `d`, the integral is a finite incomplete-gamma polynomial
and is evaluated without a remote sieve. Combining it with the exact finite
kernel sum and the pre-alias cosine envelope gives a complete universal tail
certificate for every degree-`d` field.

Using the degree-two-optimized reference window without re-optimizing for
higher degree gives

| degree `d` | `zeta(2)^d` remainder after `10^6` | worst complete bound | rounding certified? |
|---:|---:|---:|:---:|
| 2 | `1.59699e-5` | `3.90068e-4` | yes |
| 3 | `1.37121e-4` | `7.71391e-3` | yes |
| 4 | `8.39449e-4` | `1.62044e-1` | yes |
| 5 | `4.10333e-3` | `3.48969` | no |

The degree-two row uses the uniform fixed-degree remainder theorem; the
quadratic-specific alias-band theorem of Section 4 sharpens it further to
`4.56318e-5`. Degree four is the present success boundary at these parameters.
Failure at degree five means only that this universal certificate is too
coarse; it is not an impossibility theorem for degree-five recovery.

## 8. A local channel beyond Dedekind zeta

Perlis proved that, when `+/-a` and `+/-2a` are nonsquares in `Q`, the fields

\[
K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt[8]{a}),\qquad
K'=\mathbb Q(\sqrt[8]{16a})
\]

are nonisomorphic but arithmetically equivalent. Choose `a=33`. It satisfies
the nonsquare hypothesis and `33 == 1 mod 32`, so it is an eighth power in
`Q_2`. The two local algebras reduce to

\[
\begin{aligned}
K\otimes\mathbb Q_2
&\cong \mathbb Q_2[x]/(x^8-1),\\
K'\otimes\mathbb Q_2
&\cong \mathbb Q_2[x]/(x^8-16).
\end{aligned}
\]

The exact factorizations are

\[
x^8-1=(x-1)(x+1)(x^2+1)(x^4+1)
\]

and

\[
x^8-16=(x^2+2x+2)(x^2-2x+2)(x^2-2)(x^2+2).
\]

Consequently the first local algebra has component dimensions

\[
(1,1,2,4),
\]

while the second has

\[
(2,2,2,2).
\]

All components have residue degree one. The local Dedekind Euler factor is
therefore the same in both cases,

\[
(1-2^{-s})^{-4},
\]

because it sees residue degrees but not these different ramification indices.

### Theorem D — explicit zeta-blind local separation

The aggregate Dedekind-zeta response has distance zero on the fields generated
by `x^8-33` and `x^8-528`, while the enriched feature

\[
\mathcal L_2(K)=\operatorname{sort}\{[K_v:\mathbb Q_2]:v\mid2\}
\]

separates them exactly.

This is a concrete positive answer to the sixth next step: a single
ramification-aware local channel can escape a complete zeta collision. The
local algebra facts are known; the contribution here is to place the feature
inside the sensing hierarchy and verify the two polynomial identities in the
reproduction code. This is an oracle-level channel specification, not yet a
physical acquisition protocol.

## 9. What is proved, computed, and hypothesized

### Proved analytically

- reduction of continuum cosine-density extrema to Chebyshev critical points;
- Fejer--Riesz residual-to-uniform-floor implication;
- the divisor summatory upper and lower bounds in Section 3;
- the Abel-summed divisor interval bound;
- the interval midpoint-kernel envelope and alias-band tail theorem;
- coefficient domination by `d_d` and its elementary remote-tail theorem;
- separation of the explicit Perlis pair by its `Q_2` component dimensions,
  conditional only on the cited standard local-algebra facts.

### Computed and independently checked

- global HiGHS solutions of every declared finite LP;
- all critical-point extrema and coefficient-level spectral-factor residuals;
- exact Gram spectra and finite divisor-weighted held-out sums;
- million-term exact kernel sums plus analytic remainders;
- harmonic, cutoff, and sampling-ratio continuation tables;
- exact integer-polynomial products for the two local factorizations;
- unit tests comparing closed kernels, direct sums, sieves, and interval bounds.

### Hypothesized

- alias-threshold economy as a general resource-allocation principle;
- convergence of finite optimized windows to an `m`-independent continuum
  extremizer;
- further degree gains from degree-aware window optimization and sharper
  all-alias `d_d` interval bounds.

## 10. Limitations and next research targets

1. Convert the spectral-factor certificate to rational interval arithmetic or
   a proof-assistant-checkable sum of squares.
2. Derive two-sided summatory estimates for `d_d` strong enough to extend the
   quadratic alias-band method to every fixed degree.
3. Re-optimize the window for degrees four and five rather than inheriting the
   quadratic design; degree five is the first concrete target.
4. Expand the `m/T` grid near 4.413 and add an explicit sensor-noise/sample-cost
   objective. The current result concerns deterministic tail economy.
5. Model accessible enriched channels—ramification, local traces, or selected
   Artin/Dirichlet twists—and quantify their noise and acquisition costs.
6. Return to arithmetic acceleration once the higher-degree measurement layer
   is certified; multiplicativity-aware decoding may cross the current degree-
   five boundary without a stronger analytic tail.

None of these results addresses the Riemann hypothesis, changes the axioms of
numbers, or establishes an underlying physical geometry. What it does show is
more modest and concrete: logarithmic arithmetic spectra admit a useful
measurement geometry, its discretization can be controlled alias by alias,
and aggregate zeta data can be supplemented by local structure in a rigorously
testable way.
