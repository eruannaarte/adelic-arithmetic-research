# Finite Local–Global Reconstruction

## Stage 5 of the adelic-geometry program

## Abstract

The infinite synchronization theorem of Stage 4 says that conservation on all
principal elements forces every local calibration to share one scale. Stage 5
asks what survives when only bounded-height elements or bounded integer order
comparisons are visible.

Two complementary answers emerge.

- Over `Q`, bounded adjacent order data reconstructs `E(3)/E(2)` with exact
  rational certificates. The full factorization network is thousands of times
  sharper than the isolated two-prime subsystem by cutoff `100000`.
- In the quadratic laboratories, coefficient-height-two principal elements
  already synchronize every local place active at that height. Rational
  integers alone fail: field-specific elements are needed to distinguish split
  primes, and a unit is needed to distinguish the two real embeddings.

This supplies a finite, falsifiable version of local–global calibration. It
does not recover infinitely many places from one finite sample; it recovers
exactly the active finite projection and states that limitation explicitly.

## 1. Finite principal-relation criterion

Let `K` be a number field, let `X` be a finite subset of `K^x`, and let `S(X)`
contain all Archimedean places together with every finite prime occurring in a
principal divisor `(alpha)` for `alpha in X`.

For each `alpha`, form its normalized product-formula row

`r(alpha)=(log|alpha|_v)_(v in S(X))`.

All omitted finite coordinates are zero by the definition of `S(X)`, so

`r(alpha) dot 1 = 0`.                                      (1)

A candidate local-scale vector `c=(c_v)` respects conservation on the sample
precisely when

`r(alpha) dot c=0` for every `alpha in X`.                 (2)

### Proposition A (finite synchronization criterion)

The sample `X` synchronizes all active local scales up to one common scalar if
and only if its relation matrix has nullity one.

#### Proof

Equation (1) puts the all-ones vector in the nullspace. Thus nullity one is
equivalent to the nullspace being exactly its span, which says that every
solution of (2) has `c_v=c` at all active places. QED.

This is a rank criterion rather than a deep theorem, but it makes the finite
question exact and computationally testable.

## 2. Rational reconstruction and arithmetic acceleration

For `Q`, the order model uses prime-exponent vectors rather than logarithmic
product-formula rows. The finite cone is

`E(n)<=E(n+1)` for `1<=n<N`, with `E(2)=1`.

`ARITHMETIC_ACCELERATION.md` proves two reusable results:

1. the subsystem generated by `2` and `3` reduces exactly to a finite family
   of power comparisons and hence to a rational interval around `log_2(3)`;
2. a full-model bound is proved whenever a nonnegative rational combination
   of adjacent exponent differences cancels every auxiliary prime.

At `N=100000`, the resulting certified width is

`24047123/2615852168593 = 0.00000919284479785...`,

whereas the two-prime width remains `1/35`. The gain is about `3108`.

This cleanly separates two mechanisms:

- **Diophantine acceleration:** better pure-power comparisons supplied by
  continued fractions;
- **arithmetic acceleration:** additional cancellation paths through all the
  auxiliary primes present in nearby factorizations.

## 4. Exact height-two reconstruction

The smallest nontrivial examples can be proved without numerical rank.

### Gaussian field

At coefficient height two, the three elements

`1-i`, `1-2i`, `1+2i`

form a relation basis. Their norms are `2,5,5`. The two norm-five elements
generate the two distinct primes above the split rational prime `5`, while
`1-i` generates the ramified prime above `2`. Each product-formula row has one
Archimedean coordinate and one finite coordinate, with equal and opposite
nonzero coefficients. The three rows therefore force

`c_infinity=c_(1-i)=c_(1-2i)=c_(1+2i)`.

This proves exact nullity one on the four active places.

### Golden field

At coefficient height two, the three elements

`phi`, `2phi`, `1-2phi=-sqrt(5)`

form a relation basis.

- The unit `phi` has logarithmic vector `(log phi,-log phi)` and forces the two
  real-place scales to agree.
- The finite divisor of `2phi` is the inert prime above `2`; after the first
  equality, its product formula equates that finite scale with the common real
  scale.
- The element `-sqrt(5)` generates the ramified prime above `5` and equates its
  scale with the same value.

Hence exact nullity one holds on the two real and two finite active places.

This miniature basis exposes the Stage 4 mechanisms in finite form: a unit
synchronizes infinity, then principal generators attach finite primes.

## 5. Interpretation

The finite geometry is best regarded as a relation geometry.

- Places are coordinate directions.
- Principal elements are conservation hyperplanes.
- Units are rows supported only at infinity.
- Splitting creates separate finite directions that rational integers see only
  symmetrically.
- A one-dimensional nullspace is the algebraic signature of one surviving
  global scale.

This is a genuine underlying structure in number systems: it is basis-aware,
locally decomposed, globally conserved, and experimentally reconstructible.
What has not been shown is that it is a physical geometry of the universe.
Moving from the mathematical structure to physics would require an independent
observable, dynamics, and predictions that distinguish the proposal from
existing theories.
